Flags With Suns On Them

The sun is one of the most popular symbols in the world's flags — a near-universal sign of life, energy and new beginnings. More than a dozen national flags feature one, from a simple red disc to an elaborate sun with a human face. Here they all are, grouped by the kind of sun.

The most iconic sun flag: Japan 🇯🇵 — the Hinomaru, a single red disc, is the sun itself. Japan's own name, Nihon, means "origin of the sun" — which is exactly why Japan is known as the Land of the Rising Sun.

😊 The Sun of May — suns with a face

Two South American neighbours share the most unusual sun of all: one with human features.

Argentina
The golden Sun of May — a face with alternating straight and wavy rays — recalling the sun breaking through cloud at the 1810 May Revolution, and Inti, the Inca sun god.
Uruguay
The same Sun of May in the canton — 16 rays and a face — a shared emblem from Argentina and Uruguay's common history.

🔴 Sun discs & rising suns

The simplest suns of all: a single disc, or a sun climbing over the horizon for a new dawn.

Japan
The Hinomaru — a single red disc for the sun. The most recognisable sun flag on Earth.
Bangladesh
A red disc for the sun rising over Bengal, set slightly off-centre so it looks central when flying.
Niger
A small orange disc for the sun of the Sahara, between bands of land and river.
Antigua & Barbuda
A golden rising sun for the dawn of a new era.
Malawi
A red rising sun on black — the dawn of freedom and hope across Africa.
Kiribati
A golden sun rising over the Pacific, with a frigatebird soaring above.

🌟 Radiant & rayed suns

The most detailed suns, where the number of rays usually carries its own meaning.

Philippines
A golden sun with exactly 8 rays — one for each of the first provinces to revolt against Spain — plus three stars.
Taiwan
The "White Sun" with 12 rays, standing for the twelve months and the hours of the day.
Kazakhstan
A golden sun and its rays arc above a soaring steppe eagle.
Kyrgyzstan
A yellow sun with 40 rays — for the 40 tribes united by the hero Manas — around a yurt's crown.
North Macedonia
The "new sun of liberty", eight broad rays in a starburst — adopted in 1995 to settle a flag dispute with Greece.
Namibia
A golden sun with 12 triangular rays in the upper corner, for life and energy.
Rwanda
A golden sun in the top corner, symbolising enlightenment and a fresh start.
Nepal
A white sun on the lower pennant, paired with a moon above — on the world's only non-rectangular flag.

Not the sun, despite appearances — a plain disc doesn't always mean the sun. Palau and Laos both have a disc that represents the moon, and the wheel at the centre of India's flag is the Ashoka Chakra, not a sun. It's one of the most common flag mix-ups.

Why is the sun such a popular flag symbol?

The sun works on a flag for the same reasons it appears in so many myths: it's universal, instantly recognisable, and rich in meaning. A rising sun suggests a new dawn — perfect for a newly independent nation, which is why so many appear on twentieth-century African and Pacific flags. A radiant sun with counted rays lets a country encode its own story, from the Philippines' eight provinces to Kyrgyzstan's forty tribes. And a sun can carry deep cultural weight, like Japan's Hinomaru or the Inca-rooted Sun of May.

Frequently asked questions

Which flags have a sun on them?

Japan, Argentina, Uruguay, the Philippines, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Namibia, Taiwan, North Macedonia, Rwanda, Nepal, Niger, Bangladesh, Malawi, Kiribati and Antigua & Barbuda.

What is the sun on the Argentina flag?

The Sun of May — a golden sun with a face, commemorating the May Revolution of 1810 and echoing Inti, the Inca sun god. Uruguay uses the same sun.

How many rays does the sun on the Philippine flag have?

Eight — one for each of the first provinces that revolted against Spanish rule.

Is the red circle on Japan's flag a sun?

Yes — the Hinomaru. Japan's name, Nihon, means "origin of the sun", hence the Land of the Rising Sun.

Does Palau's flag have a sun on it?

No — Palau's yellow disc is the moon. So is the disc on Laos's flag, and India's central wheel is the Ashoka Chakra.
