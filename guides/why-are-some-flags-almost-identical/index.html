Why Are Some Flags Almost Identical?

Some national flags look remarkably similar. That does not always mean one country copied another — shared history, neighbouring cultures and simple flag designs can all produce similar results.

FlagAtlas Guides · Updated August 2026

The short answer:
Some national flags look remarkably similar. That does not always mean one country copied another — shared history, neighbouring cultures and simple flag designs can all produce similar results.

Chad and Romania

The flags of Chad and Romania both use three vertical bands of blue, yellow and red. Their designs are extremely similar, although the official shades differ. The resemblance is largely a consequence of the colours used by both countries in their national histories.

Indonesia and Monaco

Indonesia and Monaco both have horizontal red-over-white flags. The proportions differ: Monaco's flag is shorter and wider in its official form, while Indonesia's is taller in proportion. The two designs nevertheless look very similar.

Ireland and Côte d'Ivoire

Ireland uses green, white and orange in vertical bands. Côte d'Ivoire uses the same three colours but reverses the order, placing orange nearest the flagpole and green on the outside.

Why does this happen?

There are only so many colours, patterns and simple geometric arrangements that work well on a flag. Historical connections can also encourage countries to use similar colours. Once a design is simple enough, accidental similarities become almost inevitable.

Similarity does not mean identical meaning

Even when two flags share colours, the meanings can be completely different. A colour that represents nature in one country might represent a historical struggle or political movement in another.
