Countries That Have Changed Their Names

Countries sometimes change their official names because of politics, language, constitutional changes or efforts to reflect national identity.

FlagAtlas Guides · Updated August 2026

The short answer:
Countries sometimes change their official names because of politics, language, constitutional changes or efforts to reflect national identity.

Türkiye

In English, the United Nations uses Türkiye as the country's official short name. The change from the commonly used English form 'Turkey' was adopted internationally in 2022.

Eswatini

Swaziland changed its official name to the Kingdom of Eswatini in 2018. The change reflected the country's preferred name in the Swazi language.

North Macedonia

The country changed its official name from the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia to the Republic of North Macedonia in 2019 following an agreement with Greece.

Zimbabwe

The name Zimbabwe was adopted at independence in 1980, replacing the colonial-era name Rhodesia.

Names can matter

A country's name can carry history and political meaning just as a flag does. Changes are often intended to reflect how a country identifies itself.
