Flags With Weapons On Them

Most countries soften their founding struggles into abstract stripes and stars. A handful do the opposite — putting the weapon itself on the flag. From Mozambique's AK-47 to Barbados's trident, here is every national flag that carries a weapon, and what each one means.

The famous one: Mozambique 🇲🇿 — Mozambique's flag is the only national flag on Earth to show a modern firearm: a bayonet-fixed AK-47, crossed with a hoe over an open book. Adopted in 1983, the rifle stands for the armed struggle for independence — and it's actually written into the country's constitution.

🔫 Firearms & cannon

Mozambique
A bayonet-fixed AK-47 crossed with a hoe, over an open book — the only modern firearm on any flag.
Guatemala
Two crossed rifles and two crossed swords behind the quetzal, marking independence from Spain.
Haiti
A "trophy of arms" — cannons, muskets and flags around a royal palm — for the defence of freedom.
Bolivia
Muskets, a cannon and an axe stacked in the arms, beside an Andean condor and an alpaca.

🗡️ Swords & daggers

Saudi Arabia
A single sabre beneath the shahada — the strength and justice of the House of Saud.
Oman
The national emblem: a curved khanjar dagger sheathed over two crossed swords.
Sri Lanka
The golden lion grips a kastane, a traditional Sinhalese sword, for authority and bravery.

🛡️ Spears & shields

Kenya
Two crossed spears behind a traditional Maasai shield, symbolising the defence of freedom.
Eswatini
A black-and-white Nguni shield with two spears and a fighting staff, for protection.

🔱 A trident and a machete

Barbados
The head of a broken trident — the snapped shaft marking the break from colonial rule.
Angola
A machete crossed with half a cogwheel beneath a star — echoing the hammer and sickle.

On the arms, not the flag — Zimbabwe and Timor-Leste (East Timor) both feature a rifle on their national coat of arms, but not on the flag itself — so they don't quite make this list. Mozambique remains the only country to put a modern gun on the flag proper.

Why put a weapon on a flag?

Almost every weapon here means the same thing: a fight for freedom, and the will to defend it. Newly independent nations often chose to show the tools of their struggle rather than hide them — spears and shields for the warriors who resisted colonial rule, muskets and cannon for the wars of independence, and, in Mozambique's case, the very rifle that won it. What reads as militarism to some is, to these countries, a proud record of how hard sovereignty was won. Most of the weapons are deliberately traditional; only Mozambique's is modern.

Frequently asked questions

Which flag has an AK-47 on it?

Mozambique's — a bayonet-fixed AK-47 crossed with a hoe over an open book. It's the only national flag with a modern firearm, and it's written into the constitution.

Which flags have swords on them?

Saudi Arabia (a sword under the shahada), Oman (a khanjar dagger and two swords) and Sri Lanka (the lion's kastane sword).

Which flag has a trident on it?

Barbados — the head of a broken trident, the snapped shaft symbolising the break from colonial rule.

Why do some flags have weapons on them?

They almost always represent the armed struggle for independence or a nation's readiness to defend itself.

Do any other flags have guns besides Mozambique?

Guatemala, Haiti and Bolivia show historic rifles, muskets or cannon in their coats of arms — but Mozambique is the only one with a modern automatic weapon.
