The Largest and Smallest Countries in the World

From a country bigger than a dwarf planet to one smaller than a golf course, the world's nations span an extraordinary range of sizes. Here are the biggest and smallest countries — by land area and by population — ranked, with the facts that make each one remarkable.

Biggest of all: Russia 🇷🇺 — at 17.1 million km², Russia covers about 11% of all the land on Earth, spans 11 time zones, and is larger than the dwarf planet Pluto. It's nearly twice the size of the next country, Canada.

Surprising size facts

Big on the map isn't the same as crowded: Russia holds 11% of the world's land but only about 2% of its people, while tiny Bangladesh packs 175 million into an area smaller than the American state of Iowa. Canada has the longest coastline of any country — over 200,000 km. Kazakhstan is the largest country with no coast at all. Algeria only became Africa's largest country in 2011, when South Sudan split from Sudan. And Greenland, though the world's largest island, isn't a country at all — it belongs to Denmark, so it never appears in these rankings.

Frequently asked questions

What is the largest country in the world?

Russia, at about 17.1 million km² — roughly 11% of Earth's land, and larger than Pluto.

What is the smallest country in the world?

Vatican City, at just 0.49 km² — the smallest by both area and population.

What is the most populous country in the world?

India, with about 1.46 billion people. It overtook China around 2023.

What is the largest country in Africa?

Algeria — it took the title from Sudan in 2011, when South Sudan became independent.

Is Greenland a country?

No — it's the world's largest island, but an autonomous territory of Denmark, so it isn't in country rankings.
