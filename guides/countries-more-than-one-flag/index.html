Why Do Some Countries Have More Than One Flag?

Most of us picture a single flag per country — but many nations have several official flags, each for a different job. A plain one for citizens, a grander one for the government, special ensigns for ships, and a personal standard for the head of state.

FlagAtlas Guides · Updated August 2026

The short answer:
A country can have different official flags for different purposes: a civil flag for citizens, a state flag for the government (often with a coat of arms), naval ensigns for ships, and royal or presidential standards for the head of state. They're not mistakes or variations — each is an official flag with its own specific use.

The three main types: civil, state and war

Vexillologists (flag experts) often sort a country's flags into three families by who uses them: the civil flag flown by ordinary people, the state flag used by government bodies, and the war flag used by the military. Many countries make all three identical — one flag for everyone. But some give each a distinct design, which is how a single nation ends up with several official flags at once.

Civil flags vs state flags

The most common split is between a plain civil flag and a more elaborate state flag. The state version typically adds a coat of arms or emblem to mark government authority, while the civil version leaves it off. Spain is the textbook example:

Citizens can fly the simple red-yellow-red, while the version with the national arms is the one you'll see on government buildings and official documents. Peru, Bolivia, Venezuela and several other countries make the same distinction — plain for the public, arms-bearing for the state.

Naval flags and ensigns

Ships have their own flag traditions, and ensigns — the flags flown at a vessel's stern — are often different from the national flag on land. The United Kingdom has a famous set of three:

The UK's three ensigns: the Red Ensign (the "Red Duster") for merchant and civilian ships; the White Ensign for the Royal Navy; and the Blue Ensign for government service and naval-reserve vessels — each a coloured field with the Union Jack in the corner.

Japan shows the same idea in a different way. Its national flag is the plain Hinomaru sun disc, but its maritime self-defence force flies a separate naval ensign — the Rising Sun, with red rays added around the disc:

Royal and presidential standards

Many countries also have a flag for the person at the top. A royal standard is the monarch's personal flag — in the United Kingdom it shows the royal arms (the lions of England, the lion of Scotland and the harp of Ireland) rather than the Union Jack, and is flown wherever the sovereign is present. Notably, it is never lowered to half-mast, because the monarchy itself continues even between reigns. Republics often have an equivalent presidential standard that marks the presence of the head of state. These flags identify an office or person, not the country as a whole.

One country, several flags: the UK

Sometimes "more than one flag" simply means a country is itself made up of nations. The United Kingdom flies the Union Jack, but England (St George's Cross), Scotland (the Saltire), Wales (the Red Dragon) and Northern Ireland each have their own flags too — which is why you'll see different flags at, say, different sporting events. Why the distinction matters

All of this means that when you spot a flag that looks almost like a country's familiar national flag — but with an emblem added, or a different background — it's usually not an error. It's a specific official flag doing a specific job: telling you whether you're looking at a citizen, a government, a warship or a head of state. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a civil flag and a state flag?

A civil flag is for citizens (often plainer); a state flag is for the government and usually adds a coat of arms. Spain's plain vs arms-bearing flags are the classic example.

What are the Red, White and Blue Ensigns?

The UK's naval ensigns: red for merchant ships, white for the Royal Navy, blue for government and reserve vessels — each with the Union Jack in the corner.

Why does Japan have two flags?

The Hinomaru is the national flag; the Rising Sun, with added rays, is the naval ensign used by its maritime force.

What is a royal standard?

The monarch's personal flag, showing the royal arms and flown where the sovereign is present. It's never flown at half-mast.

Does the United Kingdom have more than one flag?

Yes — the Union Jack, the four constituent-country flags, shipping and military ensigns, and the royal standard.
