Why Do Countries Change Their Flags?

Countries change flags for many reasons — revolutions, independence, new governments, constitutional changes and attempts to create a new national identity.

FlagAtlas Guides · Updated August 2026

The short answer:
Countries change flags for many reasons — revolutions, independence, new governments, constitutional changes and attempts to create a new national identity.

Why change a flag?

A national flag is more than a piece of coloured cloth. It can represent a government, a political system, a historical tradition or an idea of national identity. When those things change, a country may decide that its flag should change too.

Revolution and political change

Revolutions are one of the clearest reasons for a new flag. A new government may want to remove symbols associated with the old regime and replace them with colours or emblems representing a new political direction.

Independence and decolonisation

Newly independent countries have often adopted new flags to distinguish themselves from a former colonial power. The new design can celebrate local history, culture, geography or independence.

Restoring an older flag

Sometimes a country does the opposite and brings back an older design. This can happen when a later political period is rejected and an earlier national tradition is revived.

Flags can change without a revolution

A flag may also be altered for less dramatic reasons. A constitution may specify a new design, a coat of arms may be simplified, or a country may adjust proportions, colours or symbols.

A flag can become part of political debate

Because flags are powerful symbols, changing one can be controversial. Supporters may see a new design as a fresh start, while opponents may see the change as erasing history.
