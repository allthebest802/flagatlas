The Most Unusual Flags in the World

Most flags are rectangles divided into familiar colours and shapes. A few break the rules — with unusual outlines, animals, weapons, astronomical details or designs that look unlike anything else.

Published 14 August 2026 · FlagAtlas Guides

Some flags refuse to be ordinary

The world's flags include dragons, birds, weapons, stars, celestial objects and even a non-rectangular shape. What makes a flag unusual is often the story behind the design.

1. Nepal — the only non-rectangular national flag

Nepal's flag is made from two joined triangular pennons rather than a rectangle. It carries a moon and sun and has a dark-blue border around its crimson field. Nepal's Constitution specifies the design and its construction. citeturn1search24

It is probably the easiest flag in the world to recognise from its outline alone.

2. Switzerland — a square flag

Switzerland's normal national flag is square, with a white cross on red. The Swiss Confederation describes it as the only national flag in the world that is square. A rectangular version is used at sea. citeturn0search7turn0search59

3. Bhutan — a dragon

Bhutan's flag features a white dragon holding jewels. The dragon is associated with the country's name, Druk Yul, commonly translated as “Land of the Thunder Dragon”.

It is an excellent example of a flag using a creature that is central to national identity rather than a simple geometric symbol.

4. Mozambique — an AK-47

Mozambique's flag is famous because its emblem includes an AK-47 rifle alongside a hoe and an open book. The objects represent different aspects of the country's history and society.

It is one of the most distinctive political symbols found on a modern national flag.

5. Brazil — a starry sky

Brazil's flag contains a blue globe with stars representing the sky, together with the national motto Ordem e Progresso. The arrangement is based on the appearance of the sky over Rio de Janeiro.

Rather than using a small number of decorative stars, Brazil turns the night sky into a major part of the design.

6. Belize — a detailed coat of arms

Belize's flag is unusually detailed. Its central coat of arms contains tools, a mahogany tree, a shield and other elements connected with the country's history and economy.

It is much more intricate than the simple geometric flags that dominate many national flag collections.

7. Turkmenistan — an intricate carpet pattern

Turkmenistan's flag has a vertical ornamental band containing five traditional carpet motifs. It also includes a crescent and five stars.

The result is one of the most detailed national flags in the world.

8. Sri Lanka — the lion flag

Sri Lanka's flag contains a golden lion holding a sword. The lion is a major symbol of Sinhala identity and gives the flag a strong heraldic character.

Why are unusual flags interesting?

Unusual flags often make the best starting point for learning. Their distinctive features give you a question to investigate: Why is Nepal shaped like that? Why does Bhutan have a dragon? Why does Switzerland use a square?

And once you know the answer, you often know something about the country's history as well.

FlagAtlas tip: don't just memorise what a flag looks like. Ask why it looks that way. That's where the interesting stories begin.
