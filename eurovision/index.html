Eurovision Song Contest

Explore Eurovision through the countries, flags and national identities that come together each year for one of Europe's best-known music competitions.

Music, flags and national identity

Eurovision brings together performers representing participating countries, making it a natural fit for FlagAtlas. The contest is not just about music — flags, languages, national colours and visual identity are all part of the Eurovision experience.

What is Eurovision?

The Eurovision Song Contest is an international television music competition in which participating broadcasters send an act to represent them. Performances are followed by voting, with points awarded by participating countries.

The contest has developed into a major annual cultural event, attracting audiences well beyond the countries that take part.

A brief history

The first Eurovision Song Contest was held in 1956. It was created during a period when European broadcasters were experimenting with new ways of connecting audiences across national borders.

Over the decades, the contest expanded considerably. More countries joined, the production became larger and the voting system evolved, but the basic idea of bringing different European broadcasting nations together through music remained.

Eurovision and flags

Flags are one of the easiest ways for viewers to identify the countries taking part. They appear in broadcasts, graphics, audience displays, ceremonies and online coverage.

For FlagAtlas, Eurovision also provides an interesting way to explore how the same national flags that appear in history, geography and everyday life become part of a huge international cultural event.

Countries and national identity

National flags

Each participating country has its own flag and visual identity, helping viewers immediately connect an act with its country.

Languages

Eurovision performances can use different languages, giving the contest a strong connection with Europe's linguistic diversity.

Culture

Performances often incorporate musical, visual or cultural elements associated with the country being represented.

International audience

The contest allows national identities to be presented to a large international audience while competing within the same event.

How Eurovision voting works

Eurovision voting has changed over the years. Modern voting includes points awarded by professional juries and public viewers, with countries unable to vote for themselves.

The familiar points — including the famous 12 points — have become one of the defining features of the contest.

The famous 12 points

Receiving "douze points" has become part of Eurovision's vocabulary. The highest available score from a voting source is awarded to its favourite song, creating some of the most memorable moments of the results show.

Eurovision Timeline

1956 — First Eurovision

The first Eurovision Song Contest takes place in Lugano, Switzerland.

1960s–1970s — Eurovision grows

The contest becomes increasingly established as a major European television event.

1980s–1990s — A wider contest

More broadcasters and countries become involved as Eurovision continues to develop.

2000s — The modern era

The contest grows into a large-scale international television production with a much bigger stage and audience.

Today — Music, flags and culture

Eurovision remains a major annual event where national identities, flags and music meet on an international stage.
