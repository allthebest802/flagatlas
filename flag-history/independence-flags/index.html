Independence Flags

How flags became powerful symbols of independence, revolution and the creation of new nations.

Why independence movements need flags

A flag can turn a political cause into a visible identity and become a symbol of an emerging nation.

The American Revolution

Revolutionary flags helped distinguish the colonies from Britain and eventually led to the Stars and Stripes.

Latin American independence

Nineteenth-century independence movements created new flags for states breaking away from Spanish and Portuguese empires.

The French Revolution as an influence

The French Tricolour demonstrated how a simple flag could represent a political nation rather than a monarch.

African independence flags

The twentieth-century decolonisation of Africa produced many new flags, with green, yellow and red especially influential.

Asian independence movements

India, Indonesia, Pakistan, Vietnam and many others adopted flags expressing national identity, political ideas or historical traditions.

Flags and decolonisation

When colonial rule ended, adopting a national flag was often one of the visible acts of sovereignty.

A flag can outlive the movement

Once independence is achieved, the flag often survives long after the political movement that created it.
