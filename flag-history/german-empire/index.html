German Empire Flag History

Explore the black-white-red flag of the German Empire, its origins in the North German Confederation and its revival in later political movements.

Flag of the German Empire

Before the German Empire

German-speaking Europe was divided into numerous kingdoms, duchies and free cities. There was no single German national flag before the nineteenth-century movement towards political unification.

The North German Confederation

The black-white-red combination was introduced for the North German Confederation in 1867. It combined colours associated with Prussia and the maritime and trading traditions of northern Germany.

1871 — the German Empire

The German Empire was proclaimed in 1871 after the Franco-Prussian War and the unification of most German states under Prussian leadership. The black-white-red tricolour became the imperial national flag.

Black, white and red

The colours became strongly associated with the imperial German state. The combination was deliberately different from the black-red-gold colours associated with liberal and democratic German nationalism.

Imperial Germany at sea

The colours were used extensively on German naval and merchant vessels. Germany's growing navy and overseas ambitions made the flag increasingly visible internationally.

The First World War

The imperial flag was used throughout the First World War. Germany's defeat in 1918 brought the monarchy to an end and changed the country's national symbols.

The end of the empire

The German Empire collapsed during the German Revolution of 1918–19. The new Weimar Republic adopted black-red-gold rather than the imperial black-white-red.

The flag after 1918

Supporters of the former monarchy and later nationalist and conservative movements continued to use black-white-red. Its meaning therefore changed from official state flag to a political and historical symbol.

The Nazi period and later use

The black-white-red colours were briefly restored alongside Nazi symbols in the early 1930s before being replaced by the swastika flag as the sole national flag in 1935. After 1945, black-white-red remained associated with particular political traditions rather than the German state.

Why the imperial flag matters

The flag illustrates a wider pattern in German history: competing colour schemes came to represent competing ideas of monarchy, liberal nationalism, democracy and later political movements.
