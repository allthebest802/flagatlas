European Flag History

How medieval symbols, dynasties, revolutions and national movements shaped the flags of Europe.

Europe before modern national flags

Medieval Europe did not have national flags in the modern sense. Banners represented rulers, dynasties, armies, cities and religious institutions.

The importance of heraldry

Heraldic colours, shields, animals and crosses provided much of Europe’s visual vocabulary.

The Nordic Cross

Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland and Iceland all use Nordic Cross designs, creating one of Europe’s most distinctive flag families.

The British Union Flag

The Union Flag combines the crosses of England and Scotland and, since 1801, the red Saltire associated with Ireland.

Revolution changes flag design

The French Tricolour showed how a simple design could represent a political nation rather than a single dynasty.

Nationalism in the nineteenth century

National movements adopted flags as symbols of language, culture, independence and political identity.

Flags after empire and independence

The twentieth century brought the collapse of empires and the creation of new European states, producing new and restored flags.

European flags today

Modern flags preserve layers of medieval, dynastic, revolutionary and national history.
