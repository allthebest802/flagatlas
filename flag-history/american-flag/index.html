The American Flag

From thirteen revolutionary colonies to fifty stars — the story of the Stars and Stripes.

Before the Stars and Stripes

Before independence, the American colonies used a variety of British and colonial flags. There was no single national flag for the colonies.

The American Revolution

The American Revolution created a need for symbols representing the new political movement. The Continental Army and navy used several flags during the struggle against British rule.

The thirteen stripes

The modern flag's thirteen stripes represent the original thirteen colonies. The stripes alternate red and white and preserve a connection with the country's revolutionary origins.

The stars

The stars represent the states of the United States. Unlike the thirteen stripes, their number has changed as new states joined the Union.

1777 — the first Stars and Stripes

On 14 June 1777, the Continental Congress adopted a national flag with thirteen stars and thirteen stripes. The stars were to represent a new constellation, although the precise arrangement was not initially fixed.

Why the stars changed

As new states joined the United States, stars were added to the blue canton. The flag therefore became a visual record of the country's territorial and political expansion.

The 50-star flag

Hawaii became a state in 1959, bringing the total to fifty. The 50-star design was officially adopted on 4 July 1960 and remains the US national flag.

What do the colours mean?

Red, white and blue have acquired many symbolic interpretations, but the original 1777 resolution did not assign a detailed meaning to each colour. It is better to distinguish later symbolism from the documented history of the design.

The flag and American identity

The Stars and Stripes has become a powerful symbol of the United States, appearing at government buildings, military ceremonies, schools, sporting events and international events.
