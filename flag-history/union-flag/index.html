The Union Flag

How the crosses of England, Scotland and Ireland came together to create one of the world’s most recognisable flags.

United Kingdom flag
EnglandScotland

England and Scotland before union

England was associated with the red Cross of St George, while Scotland used the white Saltire of St Andrew on blue.

1603 — the crowns are united

James VI of Scotland became James I of England in 1603. The kingdoms shared a monarch but remained separate states.

The first Union Flag

In 1606 a flag combining the crosses of St George and St Andrew was created. This was the first Union Flag.

1801 — Ireland joins the design

The Act of Union created the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. The flag was redesigned to incorporate the red Saltire associated with St Patrick.

The crosses within the flag

The modern design combines St George’s Cross, St Andrew’s Saltire and the red Saltire associated with St Patrick. Wales is not separately represented because Wales was already incorporated into the Kingdom of England.

Why the red diagonal cross looks uneven

The red Saltire is deliberately offset relative to the white Saltire. This counterchanged arrangement is an important part of the design.

The Union Flag today

The Union Flag remains the national flag of the United Kingdom and is used by government, armed forces, sporting organisations and citizens.
