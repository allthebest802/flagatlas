Pan-Arab Colours

The history behind red, white, black and green — colours closely associated with Arab nationalism and several modern Arab states.

What are the Pan-Arab colours?

The Pan-Arab colours are red, white, black and green. They appear in different combinations on the flags of many Arab countries.

The Arab Revolt

The modern tradition is closely connected with the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire during the First World War.

The Arab Revolt flag

The revolt’s flag combined black, green and white horizontal bands with a red triangle at the hoist and influenced later Arab national flags.

Earlier colour traditions

Red, white, black and green had appeared in different Islamic dynastic and political contexts before 1916. Their later combination became a powerful modern political symbol.

Arab nationalism

During the twentieth century, Arab nationalist movements adopted the colours to express ideas of Arab unity and independence.

Egypt

Egypt’s modern flag uses red, white and black with the Eagle of Saladin in the centre.

Jordan and Palestine

Jordan combines black, white and green with a red triangle and star. Palestine uses the same basic colour family and arrangement.

Kuwait and the Gulf

Kuwait uses green, white and red with a black trapezoid at the hoist.

What do the colours mean?

Traditional explanations connect the colours with historical Arab dynasties and periods, but the relationship is more complicated than a simple one-colour-equals-one-dynasty formula.

Pan-Arab colours versus Islamic colours

The four-colour combination has its own political history. Green also has long-standing associations with Islam, but Pan-Arab symbolism developed as a distinct modern tradition.

Pan-Arab colours today

The colours remain common across the Arab world, with each country giving the shared tradition its own national design.
