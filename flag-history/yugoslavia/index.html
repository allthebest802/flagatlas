Yugoslavia Flag History

Explore the flags of Yugoslavia, from the Kingdom of Yugoslavia through socialist Yugoslavia and the country's eventual breakup.

Flag of socialist Yugoslavia

Before Yugoslavia

The lands that later formed Yugoslavia were divided among several empires and kingdoms, including Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire. Local national movements developed their own colours and symbols.

The Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes

After the First World War, the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes was established in 1918. It was renamed the Kingdom of Yugoslavia in 1929.

The interwar flag

The Kingdom of Yugoslavia used a blue, white and red horizontal tricolour. The colours reflected the South Slavic traditions shared by several of the constituent peoples.

The Second World War

Yugoslavia was invaded and occupied in 1941. Several competing authorities and resistance movements used different flags and symbols during the war.

Tito and socialist Yugoslavia

After the communist-led Partisan victory, the Federal People's Republic of Yugoslavia was established. The national flag retained the blue, white and red tricolour but added a red star.

The red star

The red star became the clearest visual marker distinguishing socialist Yugoslavia from the earlier kingdom. It represented the communist political system and the Partisan victory.

A federation of republics

Yugoslavia consisted of six socialist republics. Each republic had its own flag, while the federal flag represented Yugoslavia as a whole.

The breakup of Yugoslavia

Political and economic tensions intensified during the late 1980s and early 1990s. Slovenia and Croatia declared independence in 1991, followed by other republics.

The final Yugoslav state

Serbia and Montenegro formed the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia in 1992. The state later became the State Union of Serbia and Montenegro before Montenegro became independent in 2006.

The legacy of Yugoslav flags

Yugoslav flags remain important historical symbols. The federal tricolour is particularly useful for understanding the country's movement from monarchy to socialism and eventually dissolution.
