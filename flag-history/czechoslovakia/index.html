Czechoslovakia Flag History

The story of Czechoslovakia's flag, from the creation of the state in 1918 to its dissolution in 1993 and the continued use of the historic design by the Czech Republic.

Flag of Czechoslovakia

The creation of Czechoslovakia

Czechoslovakia was established in 1918 after the collapse of Austria-Hungary at the end of the First World War. The new state brought together Czech and Slovak lands and needed national symbols that could represent this new political union.

The creation of the country was closely connected with the wider movement for national self-determination that followed the First World War. A new flag became an important way of giving the new republic a recognisable identity.

The first Czechoslovak colours

The historic colours of Bohemia were white and red, and these formed the basis of the first proposed Czechoslovak flag.

A simple white-over-red bicolour was familiar and historically connected with the Czech lands, but it was not distinctive enough for the new multinational state. A design that could be recognised as something more than the traditional Bohemian flag was therefore needed.

The blue triangle

In 1920, a blue triangle was added at the hoist. The new design combined the historic white and red colours with a strong blue element, creating the distinctive flag that would become associated with Czechoslovakia.

The triangle also helped distinguish the new national flag from the Polish flag, which used a similar white-and-red horizontal arrangement. The blue element gave the Czechoslovak flag a much more recognisable appearance.

The First Republic

The flag became one of the most recognisable symbols of interwar Czechoslovakia. It was flown by government institutions and became associated with the democratic First Czechoslovak Republic.

The period between 1918 and 1938 was a formative part of the country's history. Czechoslovakia was one of the parliamentary democracies created in Central Europe after the First World War.

The Munich Crisis and occupation

In 1938, the Munich Agreement forced Czechoslovakia to surrender the Sudetenland to Nazi Germany. The country's political situation deteriorated rapidly, and in March 1939 Germany occupied the Czech lands.

Czechoslovakia was dismantled. The Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia was established in the Czech lands, while the Slovak Republic became a separate wartime state. The original Czechoslovak national flag was no longer used as the flag of a sovereign united state.

Occupation and the Second World War

The wartime period produced different flags and political symbols. The Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia used its own flag, while the Slovak state used a white, blue and red horizontal tricolour.

Czechoslovak government representatives operating in exile continued to represent the idea of an independent Czechoslovakia. The restoration of the country after the war also meant the return of its national symbols.

Restoration after 1945

After the defeat of Nazi Germany, Czechoslovakia was restored as an independent state and the familiar blue, white and red flag returned.

The country soon came under communist rule. In 1948, the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia took power, beginning more than four decades of communist government.

Despite the political change, the national flag remained recognisable. Its continued use demonstrated how national symbols can survive major changes in government and political ideology.

Czechoslovakia under communism

In 1960 the country was renamed the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic. The communist state introduced new political and national symbols, but the blue triangle flag remained the national flag.

The flag therefore became associated with both the earlier democratic republic and the later socialist state. Its meaning depended increasingly on the historical period in which it was seen.

The Velvet Revolution

In November 1989, mass demonstrations known as the Velvet Revolution brought the communist regime to an end. Czechoslovakia began a peaceful transition towards democracy.

The historic national flag continued to represent the country during this transition. It became part of the visual identity of the newly democratic Czechoslovakia rather than being discarded with the communist government.

The road to separation

After the end of communism, political differences between Czech and Slovak leaders became increasingly important. Discussions about the future of the federation eventually led to an agreement to divide the country.

Unlike the violent breakup of some other European states in the same period, the division of Czechoslovakia was peaceful and became known as the Velvet Divorce.

The dissolution of Czechoslovakia

Czechoslovakia peacefully divided into the Czech Republic and Slovakia on 1 January 1993.

The federal state disappeared, but the history of its flag did not end. The two successor countries chose different approaches to national symbolism, creating an interesting final chapter in the story of the Czechoslovak flag.

Why the Czech Republic kept it

The Czech Republic continued to use the historic Czechoslovak flag. This made the flag unusual because a design created for a former federal state became the national flag of one of its successor countries.

Slovakia adopted a different national flag, using its own white, blue and red colours together with the Slovak coat of arms. The coat of arms helped distinguish it from other Slavic tricolours.

A surviving national symbol

The Czechoslovak flag is unusual in European flag history because its design survived the state for which it was created. Today, the blue triangle and white-and-red field are officially the flag of the Czech Republic.

For historians, the design provides a visual link between several different periods: the creation of Czechoslovakia in 1918, the democratic First Republic, the Second World War, communist rule, the Velvet Revolution and the peaceful dissolution of the federation.

Czechoslovak Flag Timeline

1918 — Czechoslovakia is created

The new state emerges from the collapse of Austria-Hungary and begins developing national symbols.

1918–1919 — White and red design

The historic Bohemian white and red colours form the basis of the early national flag.

1920 — Blue triangle added

The distinctive blue triangle is added at the hoist, creating the familiar Czechoslovak design.

1938 — Munich Crisis

Czechoslovakia loses the Sudetenland following the Munich Agreement and enters a period of political disintegration.

1939 — German occupation

Czechoslovakia is dismantled and the Czech lands become the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia.

1945 — Flag restored

Czechoslovakia is restored after the Second World War and the familiar flag returns.

1948 — Communist takeover

The Communist Party takes power, but the national flag remains in use.

1960 — Socialist Czechoslovakia

The country is renamed the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic while retaining its national flag.

1989 — Velvet Revolution

Communist rule ends and Czechoslovakia begins its transition to democracy.

1993 — Velvet Divorce

Czechoslovakia dissolves peacefully into the Czech Republic and Slovakia. The Czech Republic retains the historic flag.
