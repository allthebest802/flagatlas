Czechoslovakia Flag History

The story of Czechoslovakia's flag, from the creation of the state in 1918 to its dissolution in 1993 and the continued use of the historic design by the Czech Republic.

Flag of Czechoslovakia

The creation of Czechoslovakia

Czechoslovakia was established in 1918 after the collapse of Austria-Hungary at the end of the First World War. The new state needed national symbols of its own.

The first Czechoslovak colours

The historic Bohemian colours were white and red. A simple white-over-red design was therefore considered for the new state.

The blue triangle

A blue triangle was added at the hoist in 1920. The design was intended to make the flag distinctive and to represent the Slovak part of the new state as well as broader national symbolism.

The First Republic

The flag became one of the most recognisable symbols of interwar Czechoslovakia. It was used until the German occupation in 1939.

Occupation and the Second World War

Nazi Germany dismantled Czechoslovakia in 1939. The Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia and the wartime Slovak state used different symbols.

Restoration after 1945

The Czechoslovak flag was restored after liberation and continued through the communist period, including the creation of the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic.

The Velvet Revolution

In 1989 mass demonstrations brought the communist regime to an end. The historic national flag continued to represent the restored democratic state.

The dissolution of Czechoslovakia

Czechoslovakia peacefully divided into the Czech Republic and Slovakia on 1 January 1993.

Why the Czech Republic kept it

The Czech Republic continued to use the historic Czechoslovak flag. Slovakia adopted a different design with its own coat of arms to distinguish the two countries.

A surviving national symbol

The Czechoslovak flag is unusual because a design created for a former state remains the national flag of one of its successor countries.
