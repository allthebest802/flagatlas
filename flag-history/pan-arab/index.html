Pan-Arab Colours

The history behind red, white, black and green — colours that became closely associated with Arab nationalism and several modern Arab states.

What are the Pan-Arab colours?

The Pan-Arab colours are red, white, black and green. They appear in different combinations on the flags of many Arab countries and are strongly associated with Arab nationalism, independence and shared cultural identity.

The Arab Revolt

The modern Pan-Arab colour tradition is closely connected with the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire during the First World War. The revolt's flag used black, white, green and red in a distinctive arrangement.

The Arab Revolt flag

The flag associated with the Arab Revolt combined horizontal bands of black, green and white with a red triangle at the hoist. It became an important influence on later Arab national flags.

Earlier colour traditions

The four colours were not invented in 1916. Red, white, black and green had appeared in different Islamic dynastic and political contexts before the Arab Revolt. Their later combination created a powerful modern political symbol.

Arab nationalism

During the twentieth century, Arab nationalist movements adopted the colours to express ideas of Arab unity, independence and opposition to foreign domination. The exact political meaning varied between movements.

Egypt

Egypt's modern flag uses red, white and black horizontal bands with the Eagle of Saladin in the centre. Egypt's flag history includes several changes connected with the monarchy, revolution and Arab nationalism.

Syria and Iraq

Syria and Iraq have used variations of red, white and black with green elements and stars. Their flags changed repeatedly as governments and political movements changed, showing how closely flag design could be tied to Arab nationalist politics.

Jordan and Palestine

Jordan's flag combines black, white and green horizontal bands with a red triangle and white seven-pointed star. The Palestinian flag has the same basic colour family and arrangement of black, white and green with a red triangle.

Kuwait and the Gulf

Kuwait uses green, white and red with a black trapezoid at the hoist. The design is another example of Pan-Arab colours being arranged in a distinctive national pattern.

What do the colours mean?

Traditional explanations often connect black, white, green and red with different Arab dynasties or historical periods. These explanations are widely repeated, but the relationship between individual colours and particular dynasties is more complicated than a simple one-colour-equals-one-dynasty formula.

Pan-Arab colours versus Islamic colours

The colours are sometimes described simply as Islamic colours, but Pan-Arab symbolism has its own political history. Green in particular has long had associations with Islam, while the four-colour combination became strongly connected with Arab nationalism.

Pan-Arab colours today

The colours remain common across the Arab world. Comparing national flags shows how countries can share a historical colour tradition while giving it a distinct national design.
