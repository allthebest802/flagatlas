East Germany Flag History

Explore the flag of the German Democratic Republic, the Cold War state known as East Germany, and how its flag disappeared with German reunification.

Flag of East Germany, 1959–1990

Germany divided after the Second World War

After Nazi Germany's defeat in 1945, Germany was divided into occupation zones controlled by the Allied powers. The country and Berlin became central to the growing confrontation between the Soviet Union and the Western powers.

In 1949, two German states emerged: the Federal Republic of Germany in the west and the German Democratic Republic (GDR) in the east. The division of Germany became one of the clearest symbols of the Cold War.

The first East German flag

The GDR initially used the same black-red-gold tricolour as West Germany. The shared design reflected the idea that both states were German states despite their different political systems.

The colours had a much older German history and had been associated with democratic and national movements during the nineteenth century. Using the same tricolour also meant that the new East German state did not initially have a completely separate visual identity.

1959 — the state emblem is added

In 1959, the GDR added its state emblem to the centre of the tricolour. This created the distinctive East German flag that became familiar during the later Cold War.

The emblem contained a hammer, compass and wreath of grain. Adding it clearly distinguished the GDR's flag from the plain black-red-gold flag used by West Germany.

The meaning of the emblem

The hammer represented workers, the compass represented intellectuals and the wreath of grain represented farmers. Together, the elements expressed the GDR's socialist idea of an alliance between different sections of society.

The emblem also made the political character of the state unmistakable. Unlike the plain German tricolour, the flag now carried a specifically socialist symbol at its centre.

A socialist German state

The GDR was a socialist state closely aligned with the Soviet Union. Its political institutions, economy and armed forces developed within the wider Soviet-led Eastern Bloc.

The flag therefore combined two identities: the historic German black-red-gold colours and the socialist symbolism of the hammer, compass and grain wreath. This combination reflected the GDR's claim to represent a different political future for Germany.

A Cold War symbol

The East German flag became a clear visual symbol of the division of Germany and the wider Cold War confrontation between the Soviet-led East and the Western alliance.

Its appearance at official ceremonies, international sporting events and diplomatic occasions sometimes created a very visible contrast with the West German flag. The two states used similar national colours but represented sharply different political systems.

The flag at the Berlin Wall

The GDR flag was displayed on public buildings, schools, workplaces, military sites and state institutions. It became closely associated with everyday East German political identity.

After the Berlin Wall was built in 1961, the division of Germany became even more physically visible. The East German flag appeared throughout the GDR while the West German flag was used across the Federal Republic.

East Germany and international sport

The flag also became an important part of the GDR's international identity. East Germany competed separately from West Germany at many international sporting events during the Cold War.

Medals and sporting success gave the GDR opportunities to promote itself internationally. The flag was displayed alongside the national anthem at official victories and ceremonies, making it familiar well beyond East Germany itself.

1989 — the system begins to collapse

During 1989, growing numbers of East Germans demonstrated against the government and demanded greater freedom of movement and political reform. The political situation changed rapidly.

On 9 November 1989, the Berlin Wall was opened. The event became one of the defining moments of the end of the Cold War in Europe.

The GDR flag remained in use during the transition, but its political significance was changing as demands for reform, freedom and eventually German reunification grew.

1990 — the end of the GDR

The first and only free parliamentary election in the GDR was held in March 1990. Political negotiations then moved towards German reunification.

The GDR ceased to exist on 3 October 1990, when its territory joined the Federal Republic of Germany. The East German flag consequently disappeared from official national use.

What happened to the flag?

The East German flag is now primarily a historical symbol. Original flags, military examples and state emblems can be found in museums and historical collections.

Because the flag is closely associated with the GDR's socialist government, its meaning can vary considerably depending on context. For historians it is an important visual record of the division of Germany and the political system that existed in East Germany from 1949 to 1990.

The historical importance of the flag

The East German flag is a visual reminder of a state that existed for 41 years and of one of the most important political divisions of twentieth-century Europe.

Its history also shows how a flag can change meaning. The black-red-gold colours originally represented German national traditions shared with West Germany, while the addition of the state emblem turned the design into a specifically socialist symbol.

When the GDR disappeared in 1990, the emblem disappeared with it, leaving the plain black-red-gold tricolour once again as the national flag of a unified Germany.

East German Flag Timeline

1945 — Germany divided

Germany is divided into Allied occupation zones following the defeat of Nazi Germany.

1949 — GDR established

The German Democratic Republic is created in the Soviet occupation zone. It initially uses the black-red-gold German tricolour.

1959 — State emblem added

The hammer, compass and wreath of grain are added to the centre of the flag, distinguishing it from West Germany's flag.

1961 — Berlin Wall

The Berlin Wall is built, making the division between East and West Germany even more visible.

1970s–1980s — Cold War symbol

The East German flag becomes a familiar symbol at official ceremonies, international sporting events and state institutions.

1989 — Berlin Wall opens

Mass protests and the opening of the Berlin Wall begin the rapid collapse of the GDR's political system.

1990 — German reunification

The GDR ceases to exist on 3 October and its flag disappears from official national use.
