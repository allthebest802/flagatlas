East Germany Flag History

Explore the flag of the German Democratic Republic, the Cold War state known as East Germany, and how its flag disappeared with German reunification.

Flag of East Germany, 1959–1990

Germany divided after the Second World War

After Nazi Germany's defeat in 1945, Germany was divided into occupation zones. In 1949 two German states emerged: the Federal Republic of Germany in the west and the German Democratic Republic in the east.

The first East German flag

The GDR initially used the same black-red-gold tricolour as West Germany. The shared design reflected the idea that both states were German states despite their political division.

1959 — the state emblem is added

In 1959 the GDR added its state emblem to the centre of the tricolour. The emblem contained a hammer, compass and wreath of grain.

The meaning of the emblem

The hammer represented workers, the compass represented intellectuals and the wreath of grain represented farmers. The emblem therefore expressed the GDR's socialist class alliance.

A Cold War symbol

The East German flag became a clear visual symbol of the division of Germany and the wider Cold War confrontation between the Soviet-led East and the Western alliance.

The flag at the Berlin Wall

The GDR flag was displayed on public buildings, schools, workplaces and state institutions. It became closely associated with East German political identity.

1989 — the system begins to collapse

Mass demonstrations in East Germany and the opening of the Berlin Wall transformed the political situation in late 1989. The GDR flag remained in use during the transition.

1990 — reunification

The GDR ceased to exist on 3 October 1990 when the eastern German states joined the Federal Republic. The GDR flag disappeared from official national use.

The historical importance of the flag

The East German flag is now a visual reminder of a state that existed for only 41 years and of the division and eventual reunification of Germany.
