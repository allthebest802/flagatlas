About Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka is an island nation in the Indian Ocean, just south of India. Its landscapes range from tropical beaches and lowland forests to misty central mountains and dry-zone plains.

The official capital is Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte, while Colombo is the largest city and main commercial centre.

The Sri Lanka flag

Sri Lanka's flag is known as the Lion Flag. It features a golden lion holding a sword on a crimson field, bordered by gold, alongside two vertical panels of green and orange. The design combines historic, religious and modern national symbolism.

The golden lion

The golden lion is the central symbol of the flag and is based on the traditional lion emblem associated with the Sinhalese people. It represents strength, courage and sovereignty.

The sword

The lion holds a sword, a symbol of authority, sovereignty and the defence of the nation.

The four bo leaves

Four bo leaves appear around the lion. They reflect Buddhism's deep historical importance in Sri Lanka and are traditionally associated with loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy and equanimity.

Orange and green panels

The orange and green panels represent important communities within modern Sri Lanka. Orange is traditionally associated with the Tamil community and green with the Muslim community.

The maroon field and gold border

The large maroon/crimson field contains the lion and represents the historic Sinhala identity. The gold border frames the design and unifies the flag's symbols.

Why the flag is distinctive

Sri Lanka's flag is one of the world's most detailed national flags. Rather than relying on simple stripes, it combines a heraldic lion, sword, leaves, border and community colours.

A short history

Sri Lanka has a history stretching back thousands of years. Ancient kingdoms such as Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa developed sophisticated irrigation systems and monumental Buddhist architecture. The island later came under Portuguese, Dutch and British control before independence in 1948. In 1972 it became the Republic of Sri Lanka.

History timeline

3rd century BCE — Buddhism arrives

Sri Lankan tradition records the arrival of Buddhism during the reign of King Devanampiya Tissa.

3rd century BCE–10th century CE — Anuradhapura

Anuradhapura became a major political and religious centre, famous for stupas and sophisticated irrigation works.

11th–13th centuries — Polonnaruwa

Polonnaruwa became a major royal capital with reservoirs, temples and monumental architecture.

1505 — Portuguese influence

Portuguese forces established a growing presence on the coast.

1658 — Dutch rule

The Dutch displaced the Portuguese from most coastal possessions.

1796 — British control expands

Britain took former Dutch territories during the Napoleonic Wars.

1815 — Kandyan Kingdom falls

Britain took control of the Kandyan Kingdom, bringing the island under British rule.

1948 — Independence

Ceylon became independent from Britain.

1972 — Republic of Sri Lanka

The country became a republic and adopted the name Sri Lanka.

1983–2009 — Civil war

A long armed conflict took place between the Sri Lankan government and the LTTE.

2004 — Indian Ocean tsunami

A devastating tsunami struck the island, causing enormous loss of life and destruction along the coast.

2022 — Economic crisis

A severe economic and political crisis caused shortages, protests and a change of government.

Today

Sri Lanka remains a diverse South Asian democracy with important tourism, agricultural and service industries.

Colombo

Colombo is Sri Lanka's largest city and principal commercial centre, with a busy port, colonial-era buildings, markets and modern development.

Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte

Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte is the official legislative capital of Sri Lanka. Parliament is located here, while Colombo remains the dominant commercial city.

Kandy

Kandy is a historic inland city surrounded by hills and home to the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, one of the country's most important Buddhist sites.

Sigiriya

Sigiriya is an ancient rock fortress rising dramatically above the surrounding plains. Its ruins, gardens and frescoes make it one of Sri Lanka's most famous archaeological sites.

Climate

Sri Lanka has a tropical climate influenced by two monsoon systems. Different rainfall patterns create varied conditions across the island.

Central highlands

The central highlands contain cool, misty mountains, tea plantations and dramatic scenery. Pidurutalagala is the island's highest mountain.

Wildlife

Sri Lanka is exceptionally rich in wildlife, including Asian elephants, leopards, sloth bears, sambar deer, crocodiles and hundreds of bird species.

Sri Lankan elephant

The Sri Lankan elephant is a subspecies of Asian elephant and an important national symbol. Large herds can be seen in several national parks.

Sri Lankan leopard

The Sri Lankan leopard is the island's distinctive leopard subspecies and an apex predator in protected areas such as Yala National Park.

Yala National Park

Yala National Park is famous for leopards, elephants, sloth bears, crocodiles and a huge variety of birds.

Economy

Sri Lanka has a mixed economy with important sectors including tourism, tea, apparel, agriculture, manufacturing, services and overseas remittances.

Tea

Sri Lanka is famous worldwide for Ceylon tea. Tea plantations cover large areas of the central highlands.

Cinnamon

Sri Lanka is historically associated with Ceylon cinnamon, one of the island's best-known agricultural exports.

Tourism

Visitors come for beaches, wildlife, ancient cities, Buddhist temples, tea country, surfing and mountain scenery.

Food

Sri Lankan cuisine is known for bold flavours and extensive use of rice, coconut, chilli, curry leaves and spices. Popular dishes include rice and curry, hoppers, kottu roti and dhal.

Kottu roti

Kottu roti is a popular Sri Lankan street food made by chopping flatbread on a hot griddle and mixing it with vegetables, egg, meat or other ingredients.

Religion

Sri Lanka is religiously diverse. Buddhism is the largest religion, while Hinduism, Islam and Christianity are also practised.

Languages

Sri Lanka has two official languages: Sinhala and Tamil. English is widely used as a link language and remains important in business, education and tourism.

Music and dance

Sri Lankan traditions include distinctive drumming and dance forms. Kandyan dance is particularly famous and is associated with the island's central highlands.

Sport

Cricket is hugely popular in Sri Lanka. The national team won the 1996 Cricket World Cup.

Beaches

Sri Lanka has hundreds of kilometres of coastline with beaches popular for swimming, surfing, diving and whale watching.

The Indian Ocean

Sri Lanka's position near major Indian Ocean shipping routes has given it strategic importance for centuries and shaped its history of trade and cultural exchange.

Why is Sri Lanka's flag interesting?

Sri Lanka's flag is effectively a small piece of national history in visual form. The lion, sword, four bo leaves and community panels combine religious, historical and modern national symbolism in a highly distinctive design.
