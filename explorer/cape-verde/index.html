Cape Verde Flag

Cabo Verde, also known as Cape Verde, is an island country in the Atlantic Ocean off the coast of West Africa. Its striking flag uses deep blue, white and red bands with ten golden stars representing the country's main islands. The islands are volcanic, culturally distinctive and closely connected to the sea.

About Cabo Verde

Cabo Verde is an island country made up of ten main islands and several smaller islets in the Atlantic Ocean, roughly 570 kilometres west of Senegal. The islands are divided into the Barlavento and Sotavento groups.

Praia, on Santiago Island, is the capital and largest political centre. Cabo Verde is known for its volcanic landscapes, dry climate, Atlantic beaches, distinctive Creole culture and strong links with the wider Portuguese-speaking world.

The flag of Cabo Verde

The national flag has a large blue field crossed by three horizontal stripes: white, red and white. Ten yellow five-pointed stars form a circle near the hoist.

The ten stars represent the ten main islands.
The stars are arranged in a circle rather than simply placed in a straight line, giving the flag its distinctive appearance.

The present flag was adopted in 1992 as part of the country's transition to a multi-party political system.

What do the colours on Cabo Verde's flag mean?

Blue represents the sea and sky surrounding the islands. White represents peace, while red represents effort and the determination involved in building the country.

The design reflects Cabo Verde's identity as an Atlantic island nation rather than using a conventional continental African flag pattern.

What do the ten stars mean?

The ten yellow stars represent the ten main islands of Cabo Verde. They are arranged in a circle, symbolising the unity of the islands that make up the country.

The ten islands are Santiago, Santo Antão, São Vicente, São Nicolau, Sal, Boa Vista, Maio, Fogo, Brava and Santa Luzia. Santa Luzia is uninhabited but is counted among the main islands.

Why are the stars arranged in a circle?

The circle gives the flag a strong visual representation of unity. Rather than placing the islands in a geographical outline, the design brings the ten stars together as a single national symbol.

The arrangement also helps distinguish the Cabo Verde flag from other blue, white and red national flags.

A short history of Cabo Verde

The islands were uninhabited when Portuguese explorers reached them in the fifteenth century. Portugal established settlements and Cabo Verde became an important centre in the Atlantic trade network.

The islands later became an important stopping point for ships travelling between Europe, Africa and the Americas. The history of Cabo Verde is therefore closely connected with the wider history of the Atlantic world and the transatlantic slave trade.

Cabo Verde became independent from Portugal on 5 July 1975. It initially formed a political union with Guinea-Bissau under the PAIGC before becoming a separate state.

In 1991, the country held multi-party elections. The present flag was adopted in 1992 and replaced the earlier Pan-African-inspired flag.

The old flag of Cabo Verde

After independence in 1975, Cabo Verde used a flag with red, yellow and green horizontal bands and a vertical red panel containing symbols associated with the ruling independence movement.

The design was closely related to the flag of Guinea-Bissau because both countries emerged from the same anti-colonial political movement. Cabo Verde adopted its current, more distinctive design in 1992.

Praia — the capital

Praia is located on Santiago, the largest and most populous island. It is the political, administrative and economic centre of Cabo Verde.

The historic Plateau district contains government buildings, churches, squares and other reminders of the city's Portuguese colonial heritage.

Volcanoes and Pico do Fogo

Cabo Verde is volcanic in origin. The islands were built by volcanic activity on the Atlantic Ocean floor and many retain dramatic volcanic landscapes.

Pico do Fogo, on Fogo Island, is the country's highest point at 2,829 metres. It is an active volcano and has erupted several times in recorded history.

Fogo and the volcanic landscape

The interior of Fogo is dominated by the large volcanic caldera of Chã das Caldeiras. Communities have traditionally farmed fertile volcanic soils inside the caldera despite the risk of eruptions.

Coffee, grapes and other crops are cultivated in parts of the volcanic landscape, creating agricultural traditions that are unusual for such a dry island country.

Cabo Verde's dry climate

Cabo Verde has a generally dry climate because the islands lie in the tropical Atlantic near the Sahel. Rainfall is highly seasonal and varies significantly from island to island.

Water scarcity has historically been one of the country's biggest environmental challenges. Modern infrastructure, desalination and careful water management are increasingly important.

Sea, beaches and tourism

Tourism is one of the most important parts of Cabo Verde's modern economy. Islands such as Sal and Boa Vista are known for sandy beaches, clear water and watersports.

The islands also offer very different experiences: mountainous Santo Antão is famous for hiking, while Fogo is known for volcanic scenery and Santiago for history and culture.

Cabo Verdean culture

Cabo Verde has a distinctive Creole culture created through centuries of interaction between African and Portuguese influences. The majority of people are of mixed African and European ancestry.

Cape Verdean Creole, known locally as Kriolu, is widely spoken alongside Portuguese. Different islands have their own varieties of Creole.

Music — morna and Cesária Évora

Morna is one of Cabo Verde's best-known musical traditions. It is often associated with themes of longing, love, separation and life at sea.

The singer Cesária Évora became the country's most famous international musical ambassador. Her recordings helped bring Cape Verdean music to audiences around the world.

Migration and the Cabo Verdean diaspora

Cabo Verde has a large diaspora, with many people of Cabo Verdean origin living in countries including Portugal, the United States, France and the Netherlands.

Remittances from people living abroad are important to the economy and help maintain strong family and cultural connections between the islands and communities overseas.

Cabo Verde today

Cabo Verde is a stable multi-party democracy and is often regarded as one of Africa's stronger examples of democratic governance. The economy depends heavily on services, tourism, remittances and overseas links.

The country faces challenges including water scarcity, dependence on imports, exposure to climate change and the limited natural resources of its small islands.

Why is Cabo Verde's flag interesting?

Cabo Verde's flag tells a story of an island nation rather than relying on a traditional African land-based design. The blue field dominates because the Atlantic Ocean is fundamental to the country's geography and history.

The ten stars then turn the country's geography into a national symbol, while the change from the post-independence flag to the current design reflects Cabo Verde's political development.
