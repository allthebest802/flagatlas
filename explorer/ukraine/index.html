About Ukraine

Ukraine is a large country in Eastern Europe, stretching from the Carpathian Mountains to the Black Sea. It has a long and complex history shaped by Kyivan Rus', the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Cossacks, the Russian and Austro-Hungarian empires, the Soviet Union and its independence in 1991.

Ukraine is known for its fertile plains, historic cities, Black Sea coastline, Carpathian Mountains and rich cultural traditions. Kyiv is the capital and largest city.

The flag of Ukraine

Ukraine's national flag consists of two equal horizontal bands of blue over yellow. The modern national flag was restored and officially adopted following Ukraine's independence from the Soviet Union in 1991.

The familiar interpretation connects the blue with the sky and the yellow with Ukraine's golden wheat fields. These colours have become one of the strongest visual symbols of Ukrainian national identity.

Why are the colours blue and yellow?

The blue-and-yellow combination has deep historical roots in Ukrainian national movements. The colours are also commonly associated with the country's landscape: blue skies above golden agricultural fields. The symbolism is especially powerful because Ukraine has some of Europe's most fertile agricultural land.

Ukraine's flag history

1918 — Ukrainian People's RepublicBlue and yellow were used prominently by the Ukrainian People's Republic during the struggle for independence following the Russian Revolution.
Soviet periodUkraine became a Soviet republic and the blue-and-yellow national flag was suppressed in favour of Soviet designs.
1990 — Flag restored in western UkraineBlue and yellow flags reappeared publicly as the Soviet system began to weaken.
1991 — IndependenceUkraine declared independence on 24 August 1991. The blue-and-yellow flag became the national flag of the independent state.
TodayThe flag is a major symbol of Ukrainian sovereignty, independence and national identity.

A short history of Ukraine

The history of Ukraine reaches back to Kyivan Rus', a medieval East Slavic state centred on Kyiv. Later, Ukrainian lands came under Lithuanian, Polish, Cossack, Russian and Austro-Hungarian rule. Ukraine experienced revolution, famine, occupation and war during the twentieth century before becoming independent in 1991.

Kyiv

Kyiv is Ukraine's capital and largest city. Situated on the Dnieper River, it has been a major political, religious and cultural centre for more than a millennium and was the heart of medieval Kyivan Rus'.

Lviv

Lviv in western Ukraine is known for its historic architecture and Central European cultural influences. Its historic centre reflects centuries of Polish, Austro-Hungarian and Ukrainian cultural connections.

The Dnieper River

The Dnieper is one of Europe's major rivers and flows through Kyiv before reaching the Black Sea. It has played an enormous role in Ukraine's settlement, transport, agriculture and energy.

The Black Sea

Ukraine has a coastline on the Black Sea, historically important for trade, fishing and ports. Odesa is one of the country's best-known Black Sea cities.

The Carpathian Mountains

The Ukrainian Carpathians contain forests, mountain valleys and traditional villages. Hoverla, at 2,061 metres, is Ukraine's highest mountain.

Chernobyl

The Chernobyl nuclear disaster occurred on 26 April 1986 when reactor 4 exploded during a test. The accident released radioactive material across large areas and led to the evacuation of Pripyat and surrounding communities.

Geography and climate

Ukraine has extensive fertile plains, forests, wetlands, mountains and steppe. Its climate is mostly temperate continental, with colder winters inland and warmer conditions towards the south and Black Sea.

Wildlife

Ukraine's habitats support wolves, lynx, deer, wild boar, brown bears in the Carpathians, steppe species and many migratory birds. Forests, wetlands and protected areas are important for biodiversity.

The Ukrainian language

Ukrainian is the official language and is an East Slavic language closely related to Belarusian and Russian. It uses a version of the Cyrillic alphabet and has a rich literary tradition.

Religion

Ukraine has a diverse religious heritage, with Eastern Orthodox and Greek Catholic traditions particularly important. Other Christian communities, Muslims, Jews and people with no religious affiliation are also part of Ukrainian society.

Ukrainian cuisine

Well-known Ukrainian foods include borscht, varenyky, holubtsi, deruny and pampushky. Wheat, potatoes, beetroot and cabbage feature prominently in traditional cooking.

Ukrainian culture

Ukrainian culture includes distinctive embroidery, folk music, dance, pysanky decorated eggs, ceramics and traditional dress. The vyshyvanka embroidered shirt is a strong symbol of Ukrainian identity.

Agriculture

Ukraine has exceptionally fertile soils, including large areas of chernozem, or black earth. It has long been a major producer and exporter of wheat, maize, sunflower oil and other agricultural products.

The Cossacks

The Ukrainian Cossacks were semi-autonomous military communities central to Ukrainian history and identity. The Cossack Hetmanate existed as a major political entity in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

The Holodomor

The Holodomor was the devastating famine of 1932–33 in Soviet Ukraine. Millions died amid forced collectivisation, grain requisitioning and severe repression. It remains one of the most painful events in Ukrainian historical memory.

Ukraine today

Ukraine's modern identity has been strongly shaped by its independence, democratic movements and resistance to Russian aggression. The country continues to defend its territorial sovereignty while facing the human, economic and infrastructure consequences of the war.
