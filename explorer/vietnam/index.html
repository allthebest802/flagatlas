About Vietnam

Vietnam is a long, narrow country on the eastern side of mainland Southeast Asia, with a coastline stretching along the South China Sea. It borders China to the north and Laos and Cambodia to the west.

Hanoi is the capital, while Ho Chi Minh City is the country's largest city and a major commercial centre. Vietnam is known for its rice terraces, tropical landscapes, busy cities, long coastline and distinctive cuisine.

The flag of Vietnam

Vietnam's national flag is a plain red field with a large yellow five-pointed star in the centre. The design is simple and highly recognisable.

The red background is closely associated with revolution and the struggle for independence, while the yellow star represents the unity of the country's main social groups.

Flag symbolism

Red represents the revolution and the blood of those who fought and died for independence. The central yellow star represents the unity of five main social classes: workers, peasants, intellectuals, traders and soldiers.

The five-pointed star

The five points of the star are traditionally associated with the five social groups brought together by the Vietnamese revolutionary movement. The large central position of the star emphasises national unity.

Flag history

1940 — Red flag with yellow starThe design was first associated with the Vietnamese revolutionary movement during the uprising in southern Vietnam.
1945 — Democratic Republic of VietnamThe red flag with the yellow star became the national flag of the newly proclaimed Democratic Republic of Vietnam.
1954 — Vietnam dividedAfter the First Indochina War, Vietnam was divided into North and South, with different political systems and flags.
1955 — Star proportions changedThe design of the yellow star was modified to give it its familiar proportions.
1976 — ReunificationNorth and South Vietnam were formally reunified as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam, and the red flag with the yellow star became the flag of the whole country.
TodayThe red flag and yellow star remain the national symbols of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam.

A short history of Vietnam

Vietnam has a long history stretching back thousands of years. Ancient Vietnamese societies developed around the Red River delta, while successive Chinese dynasties exerted strong political and cultural influence over northern Vietnam for long periods.

Vietnam later developed independent dynasties, expanded southwards and came under French colonial rule. The twentieth century was dominated by the struggle for independence, the First Indochina War, the Vietnam War and eventual reunification in 1976.

Hanoi

Hanoi is Vietnam's capital and one of the country's most historic cities. It is known for its Old Quarter, lakes, French colonial architecture, temples and political institutions.

Ho Chi Minh City

Ho Chi Minh City, formerly Saigon, is Vietnam's largest city and its major commercial hub. It is a centre for finance, manufacturing, technology, trade and tourism.

Geography

Vietnam stretches for more than 1,600 kilometres from north to south. Its geography includes the Red River Delta in the north, the mountains of the north and west, the narrow central coast and the fertile Mekong Delta in the south.

Ha Long Bay

Ha Long Bay is famous for its hundreds of limestone islands and dramatic karst formations rising from the sea. It is one of Vietnam's best-known natural landmarks and a major tourist destination.

The Mekong Delta

The Mekong Delta is one of the world's great agricultural regions. A dense network of rivers and canals supports rice cultivation, fruit growing, fishing and large communities in southern Vietnam.

Rice and agriculture

Rice has been central to Vietnamese agriculture and culture for centuries. The country's fertile river deltas support intensive cultivation, while terraced rice fields in mountainous areas have become famous for their spectacular landscapes.

Climate

Vietnam has a varied tropical and subtropical climate. The north has cooler winters and more distinct seasonal changes, while the south remains tropical for most of the year. Monsoon rains and tropical storms can strongly affect different regions.

Wildlife and nature

Vietnam contains tropical forests, wetlands, mountains, limestone karsts and coastal ecosystems. Wildlife includes Asian elephants, primates, bears, pangolins, hornbills and many other species, although several are threatened by habitat loss and illegal wildlife trade.

People and language

Vietnamese is the official language and is spoken by the great majority of the population. Vietnam is also home to numerous ethnic minority communities, particularly in the northern and central highlands, each with their own languages and traditions.

Culture and traditions

Vietnamese culture has been influenced by indigenous traditions as well as centuries of contact with China, Southeast Asia and Europe. Tết, the Vietnamese Lunar New Year, is the country's most important traditional celebration.

Vietnamese food

Vietnam is famous for its fresh and varied cuisine. Well-known dishes include phở, bánh mì, bún chả, spring rolls and bánh xèo. Rice, noodles, herbs, vegetables and fish sauce are important ingredients in many regional dishes.

Economy

Vietnam has transformed its economy rapidly since the Đổi Mới reforms of 1986. Manufacturing, electronics, textiles, agriculture, tourism and services have become major parts of the economy, with Vietnam increasingly integrated into global supply chains.

Vietnam and coffee

Vietnam is one of the world's major coffee producers and is particularly well known for robusta coffee. Coffee production is concentrated especially in the Central Highlands, and Vietnamese coffee culture is distinctive for drinks made with strong coffee and condensed milk.

Vietnam and cashew nuts

Vietnam is a major global centre for cashew processing and exports. The country's cashew industry is an important part of its agricultural and food-processing economy, and cashews are one of Vietnam's notable agricultural export products.

Why is Vietnam's flag interesting?

Vietnam's flag is striking because of its extreme simplicity: a single red field and one large yellow star. Unlike many national flags, there are no borders, stripes, shields or multiple symbols. The design places almost all of its meaning into the colour red and the central five-pointed star.

Vietnam and the Vietnam War

The Vietnam War remains one of the most important events in modern Vietnamese history. The conflict caused enormous loss of life and destruction, and its effects were felt well beyond Vietnam. Reunification in 1976 created the modern Vietnamese state.
