About Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe is a landlocked country in southern Africa, known for its dramatic landscapes, ancient stone ruins, wildlife and the Zambezi River. The country takes its modern name from Great Zimbabwe, the remarkable medieval stone city that became an important symbol of Zimbabwean national identity.

Harare is the capital and largest city. Zimbabwe shares borders with Zambia, Mozambique, South Africa and Botswana and has strong historical and cultural connections with the wider southern African region.

Zimbabwe at a glance

The flag of Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe's national flag has seven horizontal stripes of green, yellow, red, black, red, yellow and green. A white triangle at the hoist contains a red five-pointed star and the Zimbabwe Bird.

The design combines Pan-African colours with symbols of Zimbabwe's history and independence. The Zimbabwe Bird is based on a stone carving found at Great Zimbabwe and has become one of the country's best-known national symbols.

Flag symbolism

Green represents agriculture and the country's natural resources. Yellow represents mineral wealth. Red represents the blood shed during the struggle for liberation, while black represents the majority population.

The red star represents socialism and the country's revolutionary ideals. The Zimbabwe Bird represents the nation's history and cultural heritage and comes from an ancient stone sculpture associated with Great Zimbabwe.

The Zimbabwe Bird

The Zimbabwe Bird is one of the most distinctive elements of the national flag. It is based on soapstone bird sculptures discovered among the ruins of Great Zimbabwe. The original sculptures are believed to have had important religious or symbolic significance in the medieval society that built the city.

The bird has become a powerful national emblem, appearing on the flag, currency and other official symbols.

A short history of Zimbabwe

The territory of modern Zimbabwe has a long history of farming communities, ironworking, trade and powerful kingdoms. Great Zimbabwe flourished between approximately the eleventh and fifteenth centuries and became a major centre of trade and political power.

During the nineteenth century the region experienced major political changes and European expansion. It later became Southern Rhodesia under British colonial rule. After a prolonged liberation struggle and negotiations, Zimbabwe achieved internationally recognised independence in 1980.

Great Zimbabwe

Great Zimbabwe is one of Africa's most important archaeological sites. Its enormous dry-stone walls and enclosures were built without mortar and formed part of a sophisticated settlement that flourished during the medieval period.

The site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and gave Zimbabwe its modern name. The Great Enclosure is particularly famous for its massive stone walls and distinctive architectural design.

Victoria Falls

Victoria Falls, known locally as Mosi-oa-Tunya, meaning "The Smoke That Thunders", lies on the Zambezi River on the border between Zimbabwe and Zambia. It is one of the world's greatest waterfalls and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The waterfall stretches for roughly 1.7 kilometres and produces an enormous cloud of spray when the Zambezi is flowing strongly.

The Zambezi River

The Zambezi is one of Africa's great rivers and forms part of Zimbabwe's northern frontier. It is central to the country's tourism, wildlife and hydroelectric power resources.

Geography

Zimbabwe is largely made up of elevated plateau country, with higher areas around the central watershed and lower landscapes towards the Zambezi Valley and Limpopo basin. The country contains rivers, grasslands, woodland, rocky hills and large areas of wilderness.

Climate

Zimbabwe has a mainly subtropical climate with distinct wet and dry seasons. Temperatures generally become cooler with altitude, while the low-lying Zambezi Valley is considerably hotter.

Wildlife

Zimbabwe has important populations of elephants, lions, leopards, buffalo, hippos, crocodiles, giraffes and many antelope species. Its national parks and wildlife areas form part of some of southern Africa's most important conservation landscapes.

Hwange National Park

Hwange National Park is Zimbabwe's largest national park and is particularly famous for its large elephant population. The park covers a vast area of woodland and grassland near the country's western border.

Mana Pools

Mana Pools National Park lies along the Zambezi and is renowned for its wildlife, river scenery and walking safaris. It forms part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the Sapi and Chewore safari areas.

People and languages

Zimbabwe is culturally diverse. Shona and Ndebele are among the most widely spoken indigenous languages, while English has a major role in education, government and business. Zimbabwe recognises a broad range of official languages.

Culture and traditions

Music, dance, storytelling, sculpture and stone carving are important parts of Zimbabwean culture. The country's traditional arts include the famous Shona stone sculpture tradition, while ceremonies and community traditions remain important across many parts of the country.

Food

A staple food in Zimbabwe is sadza, a thick maize-meal dish commonly served with vegetables, meat, beans or relish. Maize is central to everyday food and agriculture, while traditional dishes vary between regions and communities.

Economy

Zimbabwe's economy includes mining, agriculture, manufacturing, services and tourism. The country has significant deposits of gold, platinum, lithium, diamonds and other minerals.

Mining and mineral wealth

Mining is particularly important to Zimbabwe's economy. Gold, platinum-group metals, lithium, diamonds and other minerals contribute significantly to exports and government revenue.

A landlocked country

Zimbabwe has no coastline. It shares borders with Zambia, Mozambique, South Africa and Botswana. Despite being landlocked, the country's rivers and its position between major southern African countries have historically made it an important regional crossroads.

Why is Zimbabwe's flag interesting?

Zimbabwe's flag is unusual because it combines seven horizontal stripes with a white triangular section containing both a star and the Zimbabwe Bird. The bird connects the modern republic directly with the medieval civilisation represented by Great Zimbabwe.
