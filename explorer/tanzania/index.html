About Tanzania

Tanzania is an East African country on the Indian Ocean, formed in 1964 through the union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar. It is famous for Mount Kilimanjaro, the Serengeti, Zanzibar and some of Africa's most spectacular wildlife.

The Tanzanian flag

The Tanzanian flag consists of a green field, blue field and black diagonal band edged in yellow. The diagonal design combines the colours of Tanganyika and Zanzibar and has become one of Africa's most recognisable national flags.

Flag colours and symbolism

Green represents the country's vegetation and agricultural resources. Blue represents the Indian Ocean, lakes and waterways. Black represents the people of Tanzania, while yellow represents the country's mineral wealth.

Flag history

The current flag was adopted in 1964, following the union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar. Its design combines elements associated with the flags of the two territories and symbolises their new political union.

A short history

Tanzania's history includes ancient communities, powerful kingdoms and centuries of Indian Ocean trade. Tanganyika later came under German and British rule, while Zanzibar developed as an important sultanate and trading centre. Tanganyika became independent in 1961, Zanzibar in 1963, and the two united in 1964.

Dodoma

Dodoma is Tanzania's official capital and the seat of the national government. It lies in the country's central region and became the designated capital in 1974, although Dar es Salaam remains the country's largest commercial centre.

Dar es Salaam

Dar es Salaam is Tanzania's largest city and principal commercial and port centre. Its name means 'haven of peace' in Arabic.

Zanzibar

Zanzibar is a semi-autonomous part of Tanzania in the Indian Ocean. It is famous for beaches, spice farms, coral reefs and historic Stone Town.

Stone Town

Stone Town is the historic heart of Zanzibar City. Its architecture reflects centuries of African, Arab, Persian, Indian and European influence and its old town is recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Mount Kilimanjaro

Mount Kilimanjaro is Africa's highest mountain at 5,895 metres. Its volcanic peaks rise dramatically above the surrounding plains and make it one of Tanzania's most famous landmarks.

Serengeti National Park

The Serengeti is one of Africa's greatest wildlife areas. It is famous for the annual movement of vast numbers of wildebeest, zebra and other grazing animals and supports major predator populations.

Ngorongoro Crater

The Ngorongoro Crater is a huge volcanic caldera containing a remarkable concentration of wildlife. The surrounding Ngorongoro Conservation Area also protects important Maasai pastoral landscapes.

Wildlife

Tanzania supports some of Africa's best-known wildlife, including lions, elephants, giraffes, cheetahs, leopards, buffalo, hippos and rhinoceroses. Its protected areas are internationally important for conservation.

The Great Migration

The Great Migration sees enormous herds of wildebeest, zebra and other animals move through the Serengeti ecosystem in response to seasonal rainfall and fresh grazing. It is one of the world's most spectacular wildlife events.

Geography

Tanzania is the largest country in East Africa by area. It includes mainland Tanzania and the islands of Zanzibar, Pemba and Mafia, and borders eight countries including Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Zambia, Malawi and Mozambique.

Climate

Tanzania has varied climates. The coast and islands are generally hot and humid, the interior is more seasonal and dry, and the mountains have much cooler conditions. Rainfall patterns vary considerably across the country.

Maasai culture

The Maasai are one of Tanzania's best-known peoples, with a distinctive pastoral culture centred on cattle, community and traditional dress. Maasai communities live across parts of northern Tanzania and southern Kenya.

Swahili

Swahili is central to Tanzanian national identity and is one of Africa's most widely spoken languages. English is also widely used in government, education, tourism and business.

Julius Nyerere

Julius Nyerere was Tanzania's first president and a major figure in African independence. He promoted national unity and the Ujamaa philosophy and remains one of Tanzania's most important historical figures.

Culture and music

Tanzania has rich traditions in music, dance, carving and textiles. Taarab music is particularly associated with Zanzibar, while modern bongo flava has become popular throughout East Africa.

Food

Tanzanian cuisine reflects African, Arab and Indian Ocean influences. Popular foods include ugali, nyama choma, pilau, chapati, samaki and mishkaki, while Zanzibar is particularly known for its spices.

Economy

Tanzania's economy includes agriculture, mining, tourism, manufacturing, construction and services. Gold is an important mineral export and tourism is a major source of foreign income.

Natural resources

Tanzania has significant mineral resources including gold, diamonds, tanzanite and other gemstones. The country is also developing its natural gas resources and has extensive agricultural land.

Why is Tanzania's flag interesting?

The Tanzanian flag is unusual because of its bold diagonal black band with yellow borders. Its colours bring together symbolism associated with both mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar following their 1964 union.
