About Sudan

Sudan is a large country in northeastern Africa, stretching from the Sahara and Sahel in the north and centre to more fertile regions around the Nile. It borders the Red Sea and has a long history connecting Africa, the Middle East and the Mediterranean world.

The Sudan flag

Sudan's flag has three horizontal bands of red, white and black, with a green triangle at the hoist. It combines Pan-Arab and Pan-African colours, giving the flag a strong connection to both Sudanese and wider regional identity.

Red

The red band is associated with struggle, sacrifice and the country's revolutionary and socialist history.

White

The white represents peace, purity and hope.

Black

The black represents Sudan and its people. The colour is also connected with the country's name and its African identity.

Green

The green triangle is associated with Islam, agriculture, prosperity and the fertile land along the Nile.

Why the flag is distinctive

Sudan's flag uses the familiar Arab colours of red, white, black and green, but combines them with a bold triangular hoist design. Its colours also connect it to the wider Pan-African tradition.

A short history

Sudan's history includes ancient Nubian kingdoms, Islamic sultanates, Ottoman-Egyptian rule, the Mahdist state and British-Egyptian administration. Sudan became independent in 1956. Since independence it has experienced repeated political upheaval, civil conflict and major changes to its borders, including the independence of South Sudan in 2011.

History timeline

c. 2500 BCE–300 CE — Ancient Nubia and Kush

The Nile supported powerful Nubian states south of ancient Egypt, including the Kingdom of Kush.

7th–15th centuries — Christian Nubian kingdoms

Christian kingdoms including Makuria, Nobadia and Alodia developed along the Nile.

16th–19th centuries — Islamic sultanates

Several Muslim states developed across Sudan, including the Funj Sultanate.

1820s — Turco-Egyptian conquest

Egyptian-Ottoman forces expanded southwards and established control over much of Sudan.

1881–1898 — Mahdist state

Muhammad Ahmad, known as the Mahdi, led a major uprising and established a state centred on Omdurman.

1898 — British reconquest

Anglo-Egyptian forces defeated the Mahdist state at the Battle of Omdurman.

1899–1955 — Anglo-Egyptian Sudan

Sudan was administered under an Anglo-Egyptian condominium.

1956 — Independence

Sudan became independent on 1 January 1956.

1989 — Omar al-Bashir takes power

A military coup brought Omar al-Bashir to power.

2003 — Darfur conflict

A major conflict began in Darfur, causing widespread displacement and humanitarian suffering.

2011 — South Sudan independence

South Sudan became an independent country after a referendum.

2019 — Bashir removed

Mass protests led to the removal of Omar al-Bashir after nearly three decades in power.

2023 — War begins

Fighting broke out between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces, producing a severe humanitarian crisis.

Today

Sudan remains affected by conflict, displacement and humanitarian need while retaining enormous historical, cultural and geographical importance.

Khartoum

Khartoum lies at the meeting point of the White Nile and Blue Nile. The wider capital area includes Omdurman and Khartoum North and has historically been Sudan's political centre.

Omdurman

Omdurman lies across the Nile from Khartoum and became the centre of the Mahdist state. The city remains one of Sudan's largest urban areas.

The Nile

The Nile is central to Sudanese life. The White Nile and Blue Nile meet at Khartoum before the river flows north towards Egypt and the Mediterranean.

Red Sea coast

Sudan has a coastline on the Red Sea, including coral reefs and important ports such as Port Sudan. The coast also supports fishing and marine ecosystems.

Climate

Sudan contains several climate zones, from desert in the north to semi-arid Sahelian regions and wetter savanna areas further south. Rainfall generally increases towards the south.

Nubian pyramids

Sudan contains more than 200 ancient pyramids, many associated with the kingdoms of Kush and Meroë. The pyramids of Meroë are among the country's best-known archaeological sites.

Meroë

The ancient city of Meroë was a major centre of the Kingdom of Kush. Its royal cemetery contains distinctive Nubian pyramids and is a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Wildlife

Sudan's varied environments support gazelles, antelope, elephants in some regions, crocodiles, birds and Red Sea marine life. Wildlife has been affected by habitat loss and conflict.

Agriculture

Agriculture is important to Sudan's economy and livelihoods. Major crops include sorghum, millet, wheat, sesame, cotton and gum arabic.

Gum arabic

Sudan has historically been one of the world's major producers of gum arabic, a natural product harvested from acacia trees and used in food, pharmaceuticals and industry.

Economy

Sudan's economy has historically depended on agriculture, livestock, gold, oil and trade. Conflict and political instability have severely disrupted economic activity.

Gold

Gold mining has become an important part of Sudan's economy, although the sector has also been affected by conflict and informal mining.

Food

Sudanese cuisine includes ful medames, kisra, asida, stews and grilled meats. Sorghum, millet, beans and okra are important ingredients in many dishes.

Ful medames

Ful medames is a popular Sudanese dish of cooked fava beans, often served with oil, onion, tomato, chilli and bread.

Religion

Islam is the dominant religion in Sudan. There are also Christian communities and traditional African religious practices.

Languages

Arabic is widely spoken, with many regional Arabic varieties. English is also used in education and official contexts, alongside numerous indigenous Sudanese languages.

Culture

Sudan has a rich mixture of African, Arab, Nubian and Islamic traditions. Music, poetry, storytelling, colourful dress and hospitality are important parts of cultural life.

Sport

Football is widely followed in Sudan. The country also has a long sporting tradition in athletics and other team sports.

Why is Sudan's flag interesting?

Sudan's flag combines four colours with powerful historical associations. The red, white, black and green palette links Sudan to both Arab and African identity, while the green triangle gives the design its distinctive shape.

Sudan today

Sudan is currently experiencing a major humanitarian and political crisis following the outbreak of war in 2023. Millions of people have been displaced and access to food, healthcare and other essential services has been severely disrupted. This recent history is an important part of understanding Sudan today.
