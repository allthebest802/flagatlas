About Yemen

Yemen is a country at the southern end of the Arabian Peninsula, overlooking the Red Sea, Gulf of Aden and Arabian Sea. It has a long history as a crossroads between Arabia, Africa and the Indian Ocean.

Sana'a is the constitutional capital and one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities. Yemen is known for its ancient architecture, mountain landscapes, traditional markets and the remarkable island of Socotra.

Yemen at a glance

The flag of Yemen

Yemen's national flag consists of three equal horizontal bands of red, white and black. It is a simple tricolour with no emblem or additional symbol.

The design is based on the colours and traditions of Arab nationalism and is closely related to the flags adopted by other Arab republics during the twentieth century.

Flag symbolism

Red, white and black are commonly described as Pan-Arab colours. Red is associated with sacrifice and struggle, white with a hopeful or peaceful future, and black with the country's difficult past and periods of conflict.

A short history of Yemen

Yemen has one of the oldest civilisations in the Arabian Peninsula. Ancient kingdoms including Saba, Qataban, Hadramaut and Himyar grew wealthy through agriculture and long-distance trade, particularly the trade in frankincense and myrrh.

Islam arrived in the seventh century, after which Yemen became an important part of the wider Islamic world. Later centuries saw the rise of different dynasties and religious states, followed by Ottoman and British involvement.

Sana'a

Sana'a is famous for its distinctive multi-storey tower houses decorated with geometric patterns and white gypsum designs. The Old City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most recognisable historic urban landscapes in Arabia.

Aden

Aden is a major port city in southern Yemen. Its position beside the Gulf of Aden made it strategically important for centuries, particularly after the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869.

Socotra

Socotra is an extraordinary island in the Arabian Sea belonging to Yemen. Its long isolation has produced a remarkable number of endemic species, including the famous dragon blood tree. The island is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and is sometimes described as the 'Galápagos of the Indian Ocean'.

Geography

Yemen has a remarkably varied landscape. The country includes high mountain plateaus, dry desert regions, coastal plains, volcanic landscapes and offshore islands. The western highlands can receive considerably more rainfall than the arid interior.

The Empty Quarter

The eastern part of Yemen reaches into the Rub' al Khali, or Empty Quarter, one of the world's largest continuous sand deserts. The desert forms part of the enormous Arabian Desert.

Climate

Much of Yemen has an arid or semi-arid climate, but conditions vary significantly with altitude. The western highlands can receive seasonal monsoon rainfall, while coastal areas are hot and humid and the interior is much drier.

Wildlife and nature

Yemen's ecosystems range from mountain habitats and deserts to coral reefs and offshore islands. Socotra is particularly famous for its endemic wildlife and plants, with many species found nowhere else on Earth.

People and language

Arabic is the official language and Yemen has a rich variety of regional dialects. Yemeni society includes diverse tribal, religious and regional traditions, with strong local identities across the country's mountains, coast and deserts.

Architecture

Traditional Yemeni architecture is one of the country's most distinctive cultural features. In Sana'a and other highland towns, tall buildings are often constructed from stone, mud brick and decorated plaster, creating distinctive patterns of windows and geometric ornament.

Food

Yemeni cuisine includes dishes such as saltah, a traditional stew often served with fenugreek foam, and fahsa, a slow-cooked meat dish. Flatbreads, rice, beans and spiced meats are also important parts of Yemeni food culture.

Coffee and Yemen

Yemen played an important role in the early international history of coffee. The port of Mocha, or Al-Makha, became famous as a centre for exporting coffee, giving the word 'mocha' its association with coffee around the world.

Frankincense and ancient trade

Ancient Yemen was a major centre of the frankincense and myrrh trade. These valuable aromatic resins were transported across Arabia and the wider Mediterranean world, helping ancient South Arabian kingdoms become wealthy.

Economy

Yemen's economy has historically depended on agriculture, trade, remittances and petroleum. Agriculture remains important for many communities, although water scarcity, conflict and economic disruption have created severe challenges.

Water scarcity

Yemen is one of the world's most water-stressed countries. Groundwater depletion, limited rainfall, population growth and inefficient irrigation have placed enormous pressure on water resources, particularly around major cities.

Yemen's humanitarian crisis

Years of conflict have caused widespread displacement, food insecurity, damage to infrastructure and major disruption to health, education and essential services. The humanitarian consequences have affected millions of people.

Why is Yemen's flag interesting?

Yemen's flag is deliberately simple, but it has an important political history. The same red, white and black colours came to represent the unified state when North and South Yemen joined together in 1990. The absence of a separate emblem also makes it visually similar to several other Arab republican flags.
