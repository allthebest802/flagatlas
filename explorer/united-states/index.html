About the United States

The United States of America is a federal republic in North America made up of 50 states, the federal district of Washington, D.C., and several territories. It stretches from the Atlantic Ocean to the Pacific Ocean and from the Arctic region of Alaska to the tropical islands of Hawaii.

The United States is one of the world's largest countries by area and population and has had an enormous influence on global politics, economics, science, technology, entertainment and popular culture.

The flag of the United States

The US flag consists of 13 alternating red and white stripes and a blue canton containing 50 white stars. The 13 stripes represent the original thirteen colonies that declared independence from Great Britain, while the 50 stars represent the current 50 states.

Flag symbolism

The colours have traditional meanings associated with the Great Seal of the United States: red represents hardiness and valour, white represents purity and innocence, and blue represents vigilance, perseverance and justice. The stars and stripes provide the clearest direct link to the country's history and political structure.

Why are there 50 stars?

There is one star for each state in the United States. The current 50-star flag was adopted in 1960, after Hawaii became the 50th state. The stars are arranged in nine alternating rows of five and six stars.

Why are there 13 stripes?

The 13 stripes represent the original thirteen colonies that declared independence from Britain in 1776. The number of stripes has remained at 13 even as the number of states increased.

Flag history

1777 — Stars and Stripes adoptedThe Continental Congress adopted a national flag with 13 stars and 13 stripes.
1795 — 15 stars and 15 stripesThe flag was changed to include Kentucky and Vermont, but further additions made a permanently increasing number of stripes impractical.
1818 — Return to 13 stripesCongress fixed the number of stripes at 13 while allowing a new star to be added for each new state.
1890 — 43 starsThe flag reflected the admission of several western states during a period of rapid expansion.
1912 — 48 starsThe flag was updated following the admission of New Mexico and Arizona.
1959 — 49 starsAlaska became the 49th state and a 49-star flag was briefly used.
1960 — 50 starsHawaii became the 50th state and the current 50-star flag was adopted.
TodayThe 50-star, 13-stripe flag remains the national flag of the United States.

A short history of the United States

The United States developed from thirteen British colonies on the Atlantic coast. Growing disputes over taxation, representation and imperial authority led to the American Revolution and the Declaration of Independence in 1776.

After independence, the new country expanded westward, developed a federal political system and experienced enormous immigration and industrial growth. The Civil War preserved the Union and ended legal slavery, while the twentieth century saw the United States become a major industrial, military and cultural power.

The 50 states

The United States contains 50 states, ranging from Alaska and Hawaii outside the contiguous mainland to the 48 contiguous states between Canada and Mexico. Each state has its own government, constitution and distinctive history and culture.

Washington, D.C.

Washington, D.C. is the national capital and is not part of any state. It contains the White House, Capitol, Supreme Court and many federal government departments and institutions.

New York City

New York City is the largest city in the United States by population and one of the world's leading centres for finance, media, culture and international business. Its landmarks include the Statue of Liberty, Empire State Building and Times Square.

California

California is the most populous US state and has one of the world's largest state economies. It is famous for Hollywood, Silicon Valley, the Pacific coast, agriculture and national parks including Yosemite.

Alaska and Hawaii

Alaska is the largest US state by area and contains some of North America's highest mountains. Hawaii is an island state in the Pacific Ocean and the only US state outside North America geographically.

Geography

The United States contains an extraordinary variety of landscapes, including mountains, deserts, forests, prairies, wetlands, glaciers and coastlines. Major mountain systems include the Rockies and Appalachians, while the Great Plains occupy a vast area of the interior.

Rivers and lakes

The Mississippi and Missouri river systems are among the most important in North America. The Great Lakes form one of the world's largest freshwater systems and border the United States and Canada.

Climate

The US has an exceptionally varied climate, ranging from Arctic conditions in Alaska to tropical conditions in Hawaii and Florida. Tornadoes, hurricanes, droughts, blizzards and wildfires affect different regions during the year.

Wildlife

The country supports a huge range of wildlife including bald eagles, bison, grizzly bears, black bears, wolves, moose, alligators and many marine species. National parks and protected areas play an important role in conservation.

People and culture

The United States has been shaped by centuries of Indigenous cultures, immigration and migration. American culture has had a major global influence through music, cinema, television, literature, fashion, food and sport.

Indigenous peoples

Indigenous peoples lived across North America for thousands of years before European colonisation. Hundreds of distinct nations and communities have their own languages, traditions and histories, and many federally recognised tribes continue to maintain their cultural identities today.

Language

The United States has no official language at the federal level. English is overwhelmingly the most widely spoken language, while Spanish is also spoken by a very large population. Many other languages are used in American communities across the country.

Economy

The US has one of the world's largest economies, with major industries including technology, finance, manufacturing, energy, healthcare, aerospace, entertainment, agriculture and professional services.

Science and technology

The United States has been a major centre for scientific research and technological innovation. American institutions and companies have played important roles in computing, aviation, space exploration, medicine, telecommunications and the internet.

Space exploration

NASA's Apollo programme achieved the first crewed Moon landing in 1969. The US has continued human and robotic space exploration through programmes including the Space Shuttle, the International Space Station and Artemis.

Sport

Popular American sports include American football, baseball, basketball and ice hockey. The National Football League, Major League Baseball, NBA and NHL are major professional sporting organisations, while the US also has a huge tradition in athletics, golf and tennis.

Why is the US flag interesting?

The US flag has evolved alongside the country itself. Its 13 stripes have remained fixed since 1818 while the stars have increased from 13 to 50 as new states joined the Union. It is therefore both a historical symbol and a visual record of the growth of the United States.
