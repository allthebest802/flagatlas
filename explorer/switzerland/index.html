About Switzerland

Switzerland is a landlocked country in central Europe, famous for the Alps, lakes, precision engineering, finance, chocolate, watches and its distinctive system of direct democracy.

The Swiss flag

The Swiss flag is a white cross on a red square. It is one of only two square national flags in the world, the other being the flag of Vatican City. The modern Swiss cross developed from medieval military banners.

Red

The red field forms the background of the Swiss flag. The exact historical meaning of the colour is less certain than the symbolism of the cross itself.

The white cross

The white cross is one of Switzerland's oldest national symbols. A white cross was used by Swiss soldiers and appears in Swiss heraldic traditions dating back centuries.

The square flag

Switzerland is unusual because its national flag is normally square rather than rectangular. Swiss civil and merchant shipping use a rectangular version with the same design.

Why the flag is distinctive

The bold white cross against a red field is one of the world's most recognisable flag designs. It also inspired the Red Cross emblem, which reverses the colours.

A short history

Modern Switzerland developed from a loose confederation of Alpine communities into a federal state. The country built a tradition of neutrality and decentralised government, while industrialisation and finance helped make it one of the world's wealthiest economies.

History timeline

1291 — Federal Charter

The traditional founding date of the Swiss Confederation, associated with an alliance between the cantons of Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden.

14th–15th centuries — Confederation expands

Additional cantons and allied territories joined the growing Swiss Confederation.

1499 — Swiss independence recognised

The Swabian War helped establish practical Swiss independence from the Holy Roman Empire.

1515 — Battle of Marignano

Swiss forces suffered a major defeat and the Confederation gradually adopted a policy of non-expansion.

1648 — Peace of Westphalia

European powers formally recognised Swiss independence from the Holy Roman Empire.

1798 — French invasion

Napoleon's forces invaded Switzerland and created the Helvetic Republic.

1815 — Permanent neutrality

The Congress of Vienna recognised Switzerland's permanent neutrality and restored the federal confederation.

1848 — Modern federal state

A new constitution transformed the loose confederation into a federal state with a strong national government.

1971 — Women gain federal voting rights

Swiss women gained the right to vote at federal level, although some cantons took longer to introduce equal voting rights.

2002 — United Nations

Switzerland joined the United Nations after a national referendum.

Today

Switzerland remains a federal, multilingual and highly decentralised democracy known for neutrality, direct democracy and economic strength.

Bern

Bern is Switzerland's federal city and seat of the federal government. Its medieval old town is a UNESCO World Heritage site and is famous for arcades, fountains and the Zytglogge clock tower.

Zurich

Zurich is Switzerland's largest city and a major global financial and business centre. It sits at the northern end of Lake Zurich.

Geneva

Geneva is an international diplomatic centre and home to numerous international organisations, including major United Nations institutions and the European headquarters of the World Health Organization.

The Swiss Alps

The Swiss Alps dominate much of the southern and central landscape. Famous peaks include the Matterhorn, Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau.

The Matterhorn

The Matterhorn near Zermatt is one of the world's most recognisable mountains. Its distinctive pyramidal shape has become an international symbol of the Swiss Alps.

Jungfrau region

The Jungfrau region is famous for dramatic Alpine scenery, glaciers, mountain railways and villages such as Grindelwald, Wengen and Mürren.

Climate

Switzerland has a varied Alpine and temperate climate. Higher elevations have cold snowy winters, while lower valleys and the Ticino region can experience much milder conditions.

Lakes and rivers

Switzerland contains many lakes, including Lake Geneva, Lake Zurich and Lake Lucerne. The Rhine, Rhône, Inn and Ticino all have important headwaters or stretches within the country.

Wildlife

Swiss mountain environments support ibex, chamois, marmots, golden eagles and red deer. Conservation programmes have helped several Alpine species recover.

Economy

Switzerland has a highly developed economy based on finance, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, precision engineering, machinery, tourism and high-value manufacturing.

Swiss banking and finance

Switzerland has a long-established international financial sector. Zurich and Geneva are major global centres for banking, insurance and wealth management.

Precision engineering

Swiss engineering is associated with precision, reliability and high-quality manufacturing. Machinery, instruments, pharmaceuticals and advanced industrial products are major exports.

Swiss watches

Switzerland is world-famous for luxury watchmaking. Brands such as Rolex, Omega, Patek Philippe and Swatch have helped make Swiss watches synonymous with precision and craftsmanship.

Chocolate

Swiss chocolate became internationally famous through innovations including milk chocolate and modern manufacturing techniques. Chocolate remains an important part of Swiss food culture and tourism.

Cheese

Swiss cheeses include Emmental, Gruyère and Appenzeller. Fondue and raclette are two of the country's best-known cheese-based dishes.

Fondue

Fondue is a classic Swiss dish in which bread is dipped into melted cheese, traditionally flavoured with wine and seasonings.

Raclette

Raclette involves melting a wheel or portion of cheese and scraping the melted layer over potatoes, vegetables and other accompaniments.

Direct democracy

Switzerland is famous for direct democracy. Citizens can vote in national referendums and use popular initiatives to propose constitutional changes.

Cantons

Switzerland is made up of 26 cantons, each with substantial powers. This federal structure is a major part of Swiss political identity.

Neutrality

Swiss neutrality has been a central feature of the country's foreign policy since the 19th century. Switzerland is not a member of NATO but cooperates with the alliance in various ways.

Languages

Switzerland has four national languages: German, French, Italian and Romansh. German is the most widely spoken, while French dominates in western Switzerland and Italian in Ticino.

Culture

Swiss culture varies significantly between regions, reflecting the country's languages and neighbouring countries. Alpine traditions, festivals, music, crafts and modern urban culture all contribute to national identity.

Sport

Skiing, ice hockey, football, tennis and cycling are popular sports. Switzerland has produced successful international athletes, including tennis stars Roger Federer and Martina Hingis.

Why is Switzerland's flag interesting?

The Swiss flag is unusually simple but historically rich: a white cross on red, normally displayed as a square. Its design has become so recognisable that the reversed colour arrangement inspired the international Red Cross emblem.
