Flag symbolism

The white, blue and red tricolour was introduced by Peter the Great in the late 1600s, inspired by the Dutch flag. The colours are traditionally linked to nobility (white), faithfulness and honesty (blue) and courage (red).

About Russia

Russia is the world's largest country by area, stretching across eastern Europe and northern Asia and spanning 11 time zones. Its landscapes range from Arctic tundra and Siberian taiga to steppe, mountains and Pacific coastline.

Moscow is the capital. Russia has a long and complex history shaped by medieval Rus', the Russian Empire, the Soviet Union and the Russian Federation.

The Russia flag

Russia's national flag consists of three equal horizontal bands of white, blue and red, from top to bottom.

The modern tricolour has roots in the late 17th and early 18th centuries and became one of the major symbols of Russian statehood and national identity.

White

In traditional Russian interpretations, white has been associated with nobility, openness and peace. The colours have had different interpretations at different times, so there is no single universally fixed symbolic meaning.

Blue

Blue has traditionally been associated with faithfulness and honesty. It also became a strong part of Russian state symbolism alongside white and red.

Red

Red has traditionally been associated with courage and bravery. It also has a long history in Russian heraldry and state symbolism.

Peter the Great and the tricolour

The Russian tricolour is closely associated with Peter the Great. He promoted naval and state reforms and adopted a white-blue-red flag for Russian ships, drawing inspiration from Dutch maritime colours.

A short history of Russia

Russia's history includes the medieval state of Kievan Rus', the rise of Moscow, the expansion of the Russian Empire, the 1917 revolutions, the Soviet period and the creation of the Russian Federation in 1991.

Across the centuries Russia expanded over an enormous area, eventually reaching the Pacific and becoming a major European and Asian power.

History timeline

c. 9th century — Kievan Rus'

A medieval East Slavic state developed around Kyiv and became an important political and cultural centre.

988 — Christianisation of Rus'

Grand Prince Vladimir adopted Christianity in the Byzantine tradition, a pivotal event in the development of East Slavic culture.

13th century — Mongol invasion

Mongol armies conquered much of Rus' and the Golden Horde became a major regional power.

1380 — Battle of Kulikovo

Muscovite forces under Dmitry Donskoy defeated a major Golden Horde army, later remembered as an important step in Moscow's rise.

1480 — Great Stand on the Ugra

Moscow's ruler Ivan III consolidated independence from the Golden Horde's authority.

1547 — Ivan IV crowned Tsar

Ivan IV, known as Ivan the Terrible, became the first ruler to be formally crowned Tsar of All Russia.

1613 — Romanov dynasty

Michael Romanov became tsar, beginning a dynasty that ruled Russia until 1917.

1682–1725 — Peter the Great

Peter introduced major military, administrative and economic reforms and expanded Russia's access to the Baltic Sea.

1721 — Russian Empire

Peter the Great proclaimed the Russian Empire after victory in the Great Northern War.

1812 — Napoleon's invasion

Napoleon's Grande Armée invaded Russia. The campaign ended in catastrophe for the invading army and became a defining episode of the Napoleonic Wars.

1861 — Emancipation of the serfs

Alexander II abolished serfdom for millions of Russian peasants, beginning a major period of social reform.

1891–1916 — Trans-Siberian Railway

The Trans-Siberian Railway was constructed across Russia, linking Moscow with the Pacific and transforming transport across Siberia.

1904–1905 — Russo-Japanese War

Russia's defeat by Japan exposed weaknesses in the imperial system and contributed to political unrest.

1917 — Revolutions

The February Revolution ended the Romanov monarchy. Later that year the Bolsheviks seized power in the October Revolution.

1922 — Soviet Union

The Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was formally established, with Soviet Russia as its largest constituent republic.

1941–1945 — Second World War

The Soviet Union fought Nazi Germany on the Eastern Front, suffering enormous military and civilian losses before Allied victory in Europe.

1957 — Sputnik 1

The Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, the world's first artificial satellite, beginning a new phase of the Space Race.

1961 — Yuri Gagarin

Yuri Gagarin became the first human to travel into space aboard Vostok 1.

1991 — Soviet Union dissolves

The USSR dissolved and the Russian Federation emerged as an independent state.

1990s — Transition

Russia moved from a centrally planned Soviet economy towards a market-based system while experiencing major political and economic upheaval.

2000s — Consolidation and energy growth

Russia experienced strong growth during much of the 2000s, supported in large part by oil and natural gas exports.

Today

Russia remains a major state spanning Europe and Asia, with a large natural-resource base, extensive territory and a significant role in international affairs.

Moscow

Moscow is Russia's capital and largest city. The Kremlin and Red Square form the historic heart of the city, while Moscow is also a major centre for government, business, science, culture and transport.

Saint Petersburg

Saint Petersburg was founded by Peter the Great in 1703 and served as Russia's imperial capital for more than two centuries. It is famous for the Hermitage Museum, canals, palaces and its location on the Baltic Sea.

Siberia

Siberia covers a huge part of northern Asia. It contains vast forests, rivers, mountains and mineral resources and is known for extremely cold winters in many areas.

The Russian Arctic

Russia has one of the world's longest Arctic coastlines. The Arctic contains important ecosystems and large deposits of oil, gas and minerals, while sea ice and climate change create major environmental challenges.

Lake Baikal

Lake Baikal in southern Siberia is the world's deepest freshwater lake and contains an exceptionally large proportion of the planet's unfrozen surface freshwater. It is also home to unique wildlife such as the Baikal seal.

The Volga River

The Volga is the longest river in Europe. It flows into the Caspian Sea and has been central to trade, transport, settlement and agriculture in western Russia for centuries.

Climate

Russia contains an extraordinary range of climates, from Arctic and subarctic conditions in the north to temperate and steppe climates farther south. Much of Siberia experiences very large seasonal temperature differences.

Wildlife

Russia supports enormous biodiversity across its forests, mountains, tundra and seas. Species include Siberian tigers, brown bears, Amur leopards, reindeer, polar bears and grey wolves.

Siberian tiger

The Amur tiger, often called the Siberian tiger, lives mainly in the Russian Far East. Conservation work has helped its population recover from historic declines, although it remains threatened.

Russian culture

Russian culture has made major contributions to literature, music, ballet, visual art, science and mathematics. The country's cultural history includes figures such as Tolstoy, Dostoevsky, Tchaikovsky and Kandinsky.

Russian literature

Russian literature is internationally famous. Writers including Leo Tolstoy, Fyodor Dostoevsky, Anton Chekhov and Alexander Pushkin have had an enormous influence on world literature.

Russian music and ballet

Russia has a particularly strong tradition in classical music and ballet. Composers such as Tchaikovsky and institutions such as the Bolshoi Theatre became internationally renowned.

Russian food

Traditional Russian cuisine includes borscht, pelmeni, blini, shchi and pirozhki. Food traditions vary considerably across the country's many regions and ethnic communities.

Borscht

Borscht is a beetroot-based soup popular across eastern Europe and especially associated with Ukrainian and wider regional cuisine. It is also widely eaten in Russia, where it has many regional variations.

The economy

Russia has a large and diversified economy with major sectors including oil, natural gas, metals, manufacturing, agriculture, defence industries and services. Energy exports have historically been especially important.

Natural resources

Russia possesses enormous reserves of oil, natural gas, coal, timber and minerals. Its huge territory gives it access to a wide variety of natural resources, although extracting them in remote environments can be difficult and environmentally challenging.

Science and space

Russia and the former Soviet Union played a major role in the development of spaceflight. Sputnik 1 became the first artificial satellite in 1957 and Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space in 1961.

Trans-Siberian Railway

The Trans-Siberian Railway is one of the world's longest railway routes, linking Moscow with Vladivostok across more than 9,000 kilometres. It remains an important symbol of Russia's enormous geography.

Russia's flag and the Dutch connection

The Russian white-blue-red tricolour is commonly linked to the Dutch flag. Peter the Great was influenced by Dutch shipbuilding and maritime practice during his efforts to modernise Russia's navy.

Why is Russia's flag interesting?

Russia's flag looks simple, but its history is closely tied to the country's transformation from an imperial power into a modern nation state. The white-blue-red tricolour has survived major political changes and remains one of the world's best-known flags.
