About Vanuatu

Vanuatu is an island country in the South Pacific Ocean, made up of more than 80 islands in the Melanesian region of Oceania. It lies east of Australia and north-east of New Caledonia and is known for volcanic landscapes, tropical forests, coral reefs and distinctive Melanesian cultures.

Port Vila is the capital and largest city, on the island of Efate. Vanuatu's population is spread across many islands and communities, with traditional village life remaining an important part of the country's identity.

The flag of Vanuatu

Vanuatu's national flag has red and green horizontal fields separated by a black-edged yellow Y-shaped band. At the hoist is a black triangle containing a boar's tusk and two crossed namele fern fronds.

Flag symbolism

The colours and symbols reflect Vanuatu's natural environment, traditional culture and history. Red represents blood, including the blood of the boar used in traditional ceremonies. Green represents the richness of the islands, while black represents the ni-Vanuatu people. Yellow represents sunlight and the light of Christianity.

The boar's tusk is a traditional symbol of prosperity and is particularly important in Vanuatu's customary culture. The crossed namele leaves represent peace and have an important place in traditional symbolism.

The Y shape

The yellow Y-shaped element reflects the geography of the Vanuatu archipelago. Its three arms can be understood as representing the main island groups of the country, while the shape also connects the flag to the map and identity of the islands.

A short history of Vanuatu

Vanuatu's islands have been inhabited for thousands of years by Melanesian peoples. European navigators reached the islands from the seventeenth century onwards, while sustained outside influence increased during the nineteenth century.

The islands became known as the New Hebrides and were administered jointly by Britain and France under an unusual Anglo-French condominium. Independence came in 1980, when the country adopted the name Vanuatu.

Port Vila

Port Vila is Vanuatu's capital and main commercial centre. It is an important hub for government, tourism, trade and transport and sits beside a large natural harbour.

Efate

Efate is one of Vanuatu's principal islands and home to Port Vila. Its coastline contains beaches, lagoons and coral reefs, while the interior includes tropical forests and volcanic landscapes.

Geography

Vanuatu forms a long chain of volcanic and uplifted islands stretching roughly north to south. The archipelago includes active volcanoes, mountains, tropical forests, coral reefs and coastal plains.

Volcanoes

Vanuatu lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire and is one of the world's more volcanically active regions. Mount Yasur on Tanna is particularly famous for its persistent activity, while Ambrym and other islands also contain major volcanic systems.

Climate

Vanuatu has a tropical maritime climate, with warm temperatures throughout the year. Tropical cyclones can cause severe damage during the South Pacific cyclone season.

Wildlife and marine life

Vanuatu's forests, reefs and coastal waters support diverse wildlife. The surrounding seas contain coral reefs, sea turtles, dolphins, reef fish and other marine species, while the islands support many tropical birds, bats and reptiles.

People and culture

Vanuatu is culturally diverse, with many island communities maintaining distinct traditions, languages and customary practices. The country has one of the world's highest levels of linguistic diversity relative to its population, with more than 100 indigenous languages spoken across the islands.

Languages

Vanuatu has three official languages: Bislama, English and French. Bislama is a creole language widely used as a common language between communities. Indigenous languages remain central to local identity.

Custom and kastom

The concept of kastom refers broadly to traditional customs, knowledge, ceremonies and social practices. Kastom remains an important part of life in many communities and exists alongside Christianity and modern political institutions.

The boar's tusk

Boar tusks have major cultural significance in Vanuatu. Traditionally, pigs can represent wealth and social status, and a circularly curved tusk is especially valued. The symbol on the national flag therefore connects the modern state directly with traditional ni-Vanuatu culture.

Economy

Vanuatu's economy relies on agriculture, tourism, fisheries, construction and services. Subsistence farming remains important in many rural communities, while tourism is a major source of foreign income.

Tourism

Visitors are attracted by Vanuatu's tropical beaches, coral reefs, volcanoes, forests and cultural experiences. Popular activities include diving, snorkelling, hiking, kayaking and visiting traditional communities.

Climate change and natural disasters

Vanuatu is highly exposed to tropical cyclones, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions and sea-level rise. Climate change is an important national concern because many communities depend directly on coastal and agricultural environments.

Vanuatu and the Second World War

During the Second World War, the New Hebrides became an important Allied base in the Pacific. Large numbers of troops and supplies passed through the islands, leaving a lasting historical legacy.

Why is Vanuatu's flag interesting?

Vanuatu's flag is unusual because almost every part of its design carries a direct cultural or geographical meaning. The boar's tusk, namele leaves, Y-shaped band and four main colours combine traditional Melanesian symbolism with the story of national independence.
