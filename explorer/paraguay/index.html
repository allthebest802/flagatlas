About Paraguay

Paraguay is a landlocked country in the heart of South America, bordered by Argentina, Brazil and Bolivia. The Paraguay River divides much of the country into eastern and western regions.

Asunción is the capital and largest city. Paraguay is known for its Guaraní heritage, bilingual culture, vast Chaco region and distinctive red, white and blue flag.

The flag of Paraguay

Paraguay's flag has three horizontal stripes of red, white and blue. It is especially unusual because the two sides of the flag carry different emblems.

Paraguay adopted the present tricolour design in 1842.
The obverse traditionally bears the national coat of arms with the lion and liberty cap, while the reverse bears the Treasury seal with a lion and the words Paz y Justicia — Peace and Justice.

The red stripe

The red stripe is traditionally associated with courage, bravery and patriotism. Red forms the upper band of the Paraguayan tricolour.

The white stripe

The central white stripe is associated with peace and unity. It separates the red and blue fields and provides the background for Paraguay's seals.

The blue stripe

The blue stripe is traditionally linked with liberty and justice. Together, the three colours form a design strongly associated with the country's independence and republican identity.

The two sides of the flag

Paraguay's flag is one of the world's most unusual national flags because its front and back are officially different.

The obverse displays the national coat of arms. The reverse displays the Treasury seal. This means that simply turning the flag over changes the central emblem.

The national coat of arms

The obverse features a circular emblem containing a yellow five-pointed star surrounded by the words República del Paraguay. The star is commonly associated with independence and the founding of the republic.

The Treasury seal

The reverse bears a lion guarding a staff topped by a liberty cap, surrounded by the words Paz y Justicia, meaning Peace and Justice.

A short history of Paraguay

Paraguay's history combines Indigenous Guaraní civilisation, Spanish colonisation, independence, devastating 19th-century wars, long periods of political instability and the return to democracy in the late 20th century.

The country has retained an unusually strong Indigenous linguistic and cultural influence, with Guaraní spoken alongside Spanish by much of the population.

History timeline

Before European arrival — Guaraní societies

The region was inhabited by Guaraní-speaking peoples and other Indigenous communities with established farming, hunting, trading and cultural traditions.

1524 — Alejo García reaches the region

One of the earliest Europeans known to enter the interior of South America reached the Paraguay region during an expedition from the Atlantic coast.

1537 — Asunción founded

Spanish explorer Juan de Salazar y Espinosa founded Asunción, which became an important centre of Spanish settlement.

16th–17th centuries — Spanish colonial period

Paraguay became part of the Spanish colonial world. Jesuit missions developed communities among many Guaraní-speaking peoples.

1609 onward — Jesuit missions

Jesuits established a network of settlements known as the reductions. They became important centres of agriculture, education, music and crafts.

1767 — Jesuits expelled

The Spanish Crown expelled the Jesuits from its American territories, ending their direct administration of the Paraguayan missions.

1811 — Independence

Paraguay peacefully broke away from Spanish colonial rule and established itself as an independent republic.

1814–1840 — José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia

Dr Francia ruled Paraguay as a highly centralised state, pursuing isolation and political independence from neighbouring powers.

1844 — Carlos Antonio López

Carlos Antonio López became president and promoted infrastructure, industry, education and international trade.

1864–1870 — War of the Triple Alliance

Paraguay fought Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay in a catastrophic war that caused enormous loss of life and devastated the country.

1870 — Post-war Paraguay

The country faced severe population loss, economic damage and territorial concessions after the war.

1932–1935 — Chaco War

Paraguay fought Bolivia over the Gran Chaco. Paraguay won most of the disputed territory.

1954–1989 — Stroessner dictatorship

Alfredo Stroessner ruled Paraguay for more than three decades, combining authoritarian government with the long dominance of the Colorado Party.

1989 — Stroessner overthrown

A military coup removed Stroessner and began a transition toward democratic government.

1992 — New constitution

Paraguay adopted a democratic constitution establishing stronger civil and political rights.

1991 — Mercosur founded

Paraguay became a founding member of the Southern Common Market alongside Argentina, Brazil and Uruguay.

2000s — Economic development

Agriculture, energy production and international trade became increasingly important to the economy.

Today

Paraguay is a democratic republic with a mixed economy, strong Guaraní cultural identity and major agricultural and hydroelectric exports.

The Guaraní people

The Guaraní are central to Paraguay's identity. Their language and cultural traditions survived Spanish colonisation and remain deeply embedded in modern Paraguayan life.

Guaraní is an official language alongside Spanish, making Paraguay unusual in the Americas.

The Guaraní language

Guaraní is spoken widely throughout Paraguay and is used in homes, communities, media, music and politics. Many Paraguayans use both Guaraní and Spanish in everyday life.

The survival of Guaraní as a national language is one of Paraguay's most distinctive cultural features.

Paraguayan culture

Paraguayan culture combines Guaraní, Spanish and other immigrant influences. Music, crafts, food, festivals and family traditions reflect this mixture.

Traditional harp music and the dance style known as Paraguayan polka are especially well known.

Paraguayan harp

The Paraguayan harp is one of the country's best-known musical symbols. It is used in traditional music and has a distinctive sound and construction.

Ñandutí lace

Ñandutí is a traditional Paraguayan lace-making art whose name comes from Guaraní and is often translated as "spider web". Intricate circular patterns are created by hand and are an important part of Paraguay's textile heritage.

Paraguayan food

Paraguayan cuisine combines Indigenous ingredients with Spanish influences. Popular foods include sopa paraguaya, chipa, mbejú, vorí vorí and asado.

Despite its name, sopa paraguaya is a firm corn-based bread rather than a liquid soup.

Tereré

Tereré is a cold infusion made from yerba mate and water, often flavoured with herbs. It is a major part of Paraguayan social life and is recognised as an important element of the country's cultural heritage.

Paraguay's geography

Paraguay is divided geographically by the Paraguay River. The eastern region is greener, more densely populated and agriculturally productive, while the western Gran Chaco is much drier and more sparsely populated.

The Gran Chaco

The Gran Chaco covers much of western Paraguay. It contains dry forests, thorn scrub, wetlands and grasslands and supports wildlife adapted to difficult seasonal conditions.

The Paraguay River

The Paraguay River runs north to south through the country and is an important transport route. It also forms part of Paraguay's natural geography and helps divide the eastern and western regions.

Climate

Paraguay has a predominantly subtropical to tropical climate. Summers are generally hot and can be very humid, while winters are milder but can occasionally bring strong cold fronts from the south.

Paraguay's wildlife

Paraguay supports diverse wildlife including jaguars, pumas, giant anteaters, armadillos, capybaras, tapirs, marsh deer and hundreds of bird species.

The jaguar

The jaguar is one of Paraguay's most iconic predators. It survives mainly in remote forests and wetlands, although habitat loss and human conflict remain significant threats.

The giant anteater

The giant anteater is found in Paraguay's grasslands, savannahs and dry forests. It uses its long snout and sticky tongue to feed on ants and termites.

Paraguay's economy

Paraguay has a strongly agricultural economy. Major exports include soybeans, beef, corn and other agricultural products.

Hydroelectricity is another major economic asset, giving Paraguay substantial energy resources.

Itaipú Dam

The enormous Itaipú Dam on the Paraná River is shared by Paraguay and Brazil. Its hydroelectric plant is one of the largest power-generating facilities in the world and is central to Paraguay's energy economy.

Yacyretá Dam

The Yacyretá hydroelectric project is shared by Paraguay and Argentina on the Paraná River. It provides another major source of electricity for the two countries.

Asunción

Asunción is Paraguay's capital and one of South America's oldest continuously inhabited cities founded by Europeans. It is the country's political, cultural and economic centre.

The War of the Triple Alliance

The War of the Triple Alliance (1864–1870) was one of the most destructive conflicts in South American history. Paraguay fought Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay and suffered catastrophic population and economic losses.

The Chaco War

The Chaco War between Paraguay and Bolivia from 1932 to 1935 was fought over the Gran Chaco. Paraguay won most of the disputed territory, although both countries suffered heavy casualties.

Paraguay and Mercosur

Paraguay is a founding member of Mercosur, the South American regional trade bloc. Its membership links the country closely with Argentina, Brazil and Uruguay.

Why is Paraguay's flag interesting?

Paraguay's flag is exceptionally unusual because it has different official emblems on its front and back. Few national flags share this characteristic.

The red, white and blue tricolour also links the flag to Paraguay's republican history, while the seals reflect ideals of independence, peace and justice.
