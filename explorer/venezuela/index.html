About Venezuela

Venezuela is a country on the northern coast of South America, facing the Caribbean Sea. It is famous for its extraordinary landscapes, including the world's highest uninterrupted waterfall, Angel Falls, the vast Orinoco River basin and the dramatic table-top mountains known as tepuis.

Caracas is the capital and largest city. Venezuela has a long history shaped by Indigenous peoples, Spanish colonial rule, independence under Simón Bolívar, and the development of one of the world's largest petroleum industries.

Venezuela at a glance

Capital: Caracas   ·   Region: South America / Caribbean   ·   Currency: Venezuelan bolívar (VES)   ·   Official language: Spanish   ·   Independence: 1811

The flag of Venezuela

Venezuela's national flag consists of three horizontal bands of yellow, blue and red. Eight white five-pointed stars form an arc across the blue band towards the hoist.

The modern flag developed from the revolutionary designs associated with Francisco de Miranda and the independence movement in Spanish America.

Flag symbolism

Yellow is traditionally associated with the country's wealth and resources. Blue represents the sea separating Venezuela from Spain, while red represents the blood shed in the struggle for independence.

The eight stars represent the eight provinces that supported Venezuela's independence in 1811. The eighth star was added in 2006 to recognise the historic province of Guayana.

Why are there eight stars?

Venezuela originally used seven stars to represent the seven provinces that declared independence. In 2006, a eighth star was added by President Hugo Chávez to represent Guayana, which had also supported the independence movement.

A short history of Venezuela

The territory of modern Venezuela was inhabited by numerous Indigenous peoples before European arrival. Spanish explorers reached the region in the late fifteenth century, and Venezuela subsequently became part of the Spanish colonial system.

Venezuela became one of the major centres of the Spanish American independence movement. Simón Bolívar, born in Caracas, became one of the most important leaders of the wars of independence across northern South America.

Simón Bolívar

Simón Bolívar was born in Caracas in 1783 and became the leading figure in the liberation of several South American countries from Spanish rule. He played a central role in the independence of Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru and Bolivia, with Bolivia named in his honour.

Caracas

Caracas lies in a mountain valley near the Caribbean coast and is Venezuela's political, cultural and economic centre. The city sits below the dramatic Ávila mountain range, now protected as a national park.

Angel Falls

Angel Falls, known locally as Kerepakupai Merú, plunges from the Auyán-tepui in Venezuela's Guiana Highlands. With a total height of about 979 metres and a continuous drop of roughly 807 metres, it is widely recognised as the world's highest uninterrupted waterfall.

The tepuis

The tepuis are enormous flat-topped sandstone mountains found mainly in southern Venezuela. Their isolated summits have distinctive ecosystems, with some species found nowhere else. They helped inspire the otherworldly landscapes described in travel and adventure literature.

Geography

Venezuela covers a large area of northern South America. Its landscapes range from Caribbean beaches and coastal mountains to the vast Orinoco Llanos, the Guiana Highlands and the Amazonian forests in the south.

The Orinoco River

The Orinoco is one of South America's great rivers and is central to Venezuela's geography. Its enormous drainage basin includes wetlands, savannahs, forests and important habitats for wildlife.

Lake Maracaibo

Lake Maracaibo in north-western Venezuela is one of the country's most important geographical and economic features. The surrounding region has been a major centre of oil production for more than a century.

Wildlife

Venezuela contains an extraordinary range of habitats and wildlife. Species include jaguars, giant anteaters, capybaras, anacondas, river dolphins, harpy eagles and numerous tropical birds. Its isolated tepuis also contain highly specialised species.

Climate

Venezuela is predominantly tropical, but its climate varies considerably with altitude and geography. Lowland areas are generally warm throughout the year, while the Andes and other highlands have much cooler conditions.

People, language and culture

Spanish is Venezuela's official language, while numerous Indigenous languages are also spoken. Venezuelan culture reflects Indigenous, European and African influences and is especially well known for its music, food, festivals and baseball tradition.

Music and traditions

Joropo is one of Venezuela's best-known musical and dance traditions. It is strongly associated with the Llanos and features instruments such as the harp, cuatro and maracas. Carnival and Christmas celebrations are also important parts of Venezuelan cultural life.

Food

Venezuelan cuisine includes dishes such as arepas, cachapas and pabellón criollo. Arepas are especially important and can be filled with meat, cheese, beans, avocado and many other ingredients.

Economy

Venezuela possesses some of the world's largest proven petroleum reserves. Oil has therefore played an enormous role in the country's economy, government finances and international trade. Other important resources include natural gas, iron ore, gold and agricultural land.

Venezuela and oil

The development of the petroleum industry transformed Venezuela during the twentieth century. Oil wealth helped fund infrastructure and public services, but heavy dependence on petroleum also left the economy vulnerable to changes in production, prices and international demand.

National parks

Venezuela has extensive protected areas, including Canaima National Park, which contains Angel Falls and numerous tepuis. Canaima is recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site because of its exceptional landscape and ecological importance.

Why is Venezuela's flag interesting?

Venezuela's flag is one of the most recognisable independence-era tricolours in the Americas. Its yellow, blue and red colours are closely associated with the revolutionary traditions of Francisco de Miranda, while the eight stars connect the modern flag with the country's independence history.
