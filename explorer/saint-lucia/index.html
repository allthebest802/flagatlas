About Saint Lucia

Saint Lucia is a mountainous island country in the eastern Caribbean, part of the Windward Islands. It is famous for its volcanic landscape, tropical forests and the dramatic twin Pitons.

Castries is the capital and largest city. Saint Lucia became an independent country in 1979 and remains a member of the Commonwealth.

The Saint Lucia flag

Saint Lucia's national flag was designed by Dunstan St. Omer. The current design was adopted at independence and later modified, with the present version dating from 2002.

It has a cerulean-blue field with overlapping black, white and gold triangular shapes. The central design is inspired by the island's famous Pitons.

Cerulean blue

The government's official description says cerulean blue represents fidelity and reflects Saint Lucia's tropical sky and its surrounding waters, including the Caribbean Sea and Atlantic Ocean. citeturn0search0

Gold

The gold represents the Caribbean sunshine and prosperity. It also gives the central design a strong visual link to the island's bright tropical environment. citeturn0search0

Black and white

The black and white elements represent the cultural influences of Africa and Europe and the two communities living and working together. citeturn0search0

The triangles and the Pitons

The central triangular forms are a stylised reference to Saint Lucia's famous twin Pitons at Soufrière. The government's description also connects the triangular design with the hope and aspirations of the people. citeturn0search0

The designer — Dunstan St. Omer

Saint Lucian artist Dunstan St. Omer designed the national flag. His design became one of the most recognisable symbols of modern Saint Lucia. The government identifies him as the designer of the national flag. citeturn0search0

A short history of Saint Lucia

Saint Lucia has a complex history shaped by Indigenous Caribbean peoples, European rivalry, plantation agriculture and the movement towards independence. Britain and France fought repeatedly for control of the island before British rule became established.

Saint Lucia gained associated statehood in 1967 and became fully independent on 22 February 1979.

History timeline

Before European settlement — Indigenous Saint Lucia

Indigenous peoples inhabited the island long before European colonisation. Historical sources record earlier names including Iouanalao and Hewanorra.

16th–17th centuries — European contact

European sailors and colonists began appearing in the region. The precise story of the island's earliest European encounters remains partly uncertain.

17th century — French and English settlement

Both France and England established settlements and competed for control of the island.

18th century — Repeated British-French conflict

Saint Lucia changed hands several times during the colonial wars of the Caribbean.

1814 — British rule confirmed

The Treaty of Paris confirmed British possession of Saint Lucia after the Napoleonic Wars.

1834 — Emancipation

Slavery was abolished throughout the British Empire, transforming the island's plantation society.

1930s–1950s — Political change

Trade unions and political organisations grew as Saint Lucians pushed for better working conditions and greater representation.

1958–1962 — West Indies Federation

Saint Lucia joined the short-lived West Indies Federation as Britain explored new forms of Caribbean self-government.

1967 — Associated Statehood

The Associated Statehood Act gave Saint Lucia internal self-government while Britain retained responsibility for external affairs and defence. citeturn0search11

1979 — Independence

Saint Lucia became an independent nation on 22 February 1979.

1980s–1990s — Tourism grows

Tourism became increasingly important alongside agriculture, manufacturing and services.

2002 — Flag modification

The national flag was modified, with the official proportions and colours defined for the modern design. citeturn0search11

Today

Saint Lucia is a Caribbean parliamentary democracy with a diversified economy and a strong international reputation for tourism, scenery and culture.

Castries

Castries is Saint Lucia's capital and main commercial centre. It sits around a natural harbour and is an important centre for government, shopping, cruise tourism and transport.

The Pitons

The Pitons — Gros Piton and Petit Piton — are two dramatic volcanic plugs rising beside the Caribbean Sea at Soufrière. They are Saint Lucia's most famous natural landmarks and are reflected directly in the national flag.

Soufrière

Soufrière lies in the shadow of the Pitons and is associated with Saint Lucia's volcanic landscape. The area includes hot springs, volcanic features and rainforest and is one of the island's major tourism regions.

Mount Gimie

Mount Gimie is Saint Lucia's highest mountain, rising to about 959 metres. The mountainous interior contains dense tropical rainforest and numerous rivers.

Climate

Saint Lucia has a tropical climate with warm temperatures throughout the year. The mountainous interior receives more rainfall than many coastal areas, supporting lush rainforest.

Rainforest

The interior of Saint Lucia contains tropical rainforest that supports a rich variety of plants, birds and other wildlife. Forest conservation is important for biodiversity, water supplies and tourism.

Saint Lucia parrot

The Saint Lucia parrot (Amazona versicolor) is the country's national bird. Conservation efforts helped this endemic species recover from a very small population, making it an important national conservation success story.

Wildlife

Saint Lucia's wildlife includes endemic birds, reptiles and tropical marine species. Its forests and surrounding waters provide habitats for a wide range of animals.

The economy

Saint Lucia's economy is strongly connected to tourism and services, alongside agriculture, manufacturing and other activities. Bananas were historically an important export crop, while tourism has become one of the country's most significant industries.

Tourism

Visitors are drawn to Saint Lucia for its beaches, Pitons, rainforest, sailing, diving, volcanic attractions and resorts. The island combines natural scenery with a strong Caribbean cultural identity.

Bananas and agriculture

Agriculture has played an important role in Saint Lucia's economy, particularly banana production. Small-scale farming continues to contribute to rural livelihoods and the domestic food supply.

Saint Lucian culture

Saint Lucian culture reflects African, European, Caribbean and Indigenous influences. Music, food, festivals, storytelling and the island's distinctive Creole heritage all contribute to its identity.

Creole culture

Kwéyòl, the French-based Creole language spoken in Saint Lucia, remains an important part of cultural life. It is especially associated with traditional music, storytelling and festivals.

Music

Saint Lucia has a lively music scene including soca, calypso, reggae and zouk. Local music is particularly prominent during carnival and community celebrations.

Food

Saint Lucian cuisine includes green figs and saltfish, seafood, plantains, breadfruit, cassava and Creole-style dishes. The food reflects the island's Caribbean and African heritage as well as its tropical agriculture.

Carnival

Saint Lucia's Carnival is one of the island's major annual cultural celebrations. Music, costumes, parades, competitions and street events bring together communities and visitors.

Nobel Prize connection

Saint Lucia has produced two Nobel laureates: Derek Walcott, who won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1992, and Arthur Lewis, who won the Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences in 1979. This is an extraordinary achievement for a small island nation.

Derek Walcott

Poet and playwright Derek Walcott was born in Saint Lucia and became one of the Caribbean's most internationally influential writers. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1992.

Arthur Lewis

Economist Arthur Lewis was born in Saint Lucia and became a major figure in development economics. He shared the 1979 Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences for work on economic development.

National Day

Saint Lucia celebrates its independence on 22 February. The date is the country's national day and is marked by ceremonies, cultural events and displays of the national flag.

Flag etiquette

Saint Lucia has an official code governing the display of its national flag. Government guidance says the flag is normally flown from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m., with specific rules for ceremonies, half-staff display and respectful handling. citeturn0search0

Why is Saint Lucia's flag interesting?

Saint Lucia's flag is unusual because its central design is not simply decorative: the overlapping triangles deliberately evoke the island's twin Pitons. The combination of cerulean blue, gold, black and white also communicates ideas of sea and sky, sunshine, prosperity and cultural unity. citeturn0search0
