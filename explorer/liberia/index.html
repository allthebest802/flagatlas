About Liberia

Liberia is a country on the Atlantic coast of West Africa, bordered by Sierra Leone, Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire. Monrovia is the capital and largest city.

Liberia has a distinctive history as a republic founded by formerly enslaved and free Black people from the United States. It declared independence in 1847 and is one of Africa's oldest modern republics.

The flag of Liberia

Liberia's flag has 11 alternating red and white stripes, with a blue canton containing a single white star.

The flag deliberately resembles the flag of the United States.
The 11 stripes are associated with the 11 signatories of Liberia's Declaration of Independence, while the white star represents Liberia as an independent republic and the blue canton reflects the country's African setting.

The flag was adopted in 1847 when Liberia declared independence.

The 11 stripes

The flag's 11 red and white stripes are traditionally associated with the eleven signatories of Liberia's Declaration of Independence.

The design strongly echoes the American flag, reflecting Liberia's historical links with the United States.

The white star

The single white star symbolises Liberia's independence and freedom. It replaced the multiple stars of the United States flag with one star representing the new African republic.

The star makes Liberia's flag distinctive despite its obvious American influence.

The blue canton

The blue canton contains the white star and is often interpreted as representing the African continent or Liberia's place among independent nations.

Its position at the upper hoist makes the flag instantly recognisable.

Why does Liberia's flag look like the American flag?

Liberia's flag was deliberately based on the United States flag. The country's founding was closely connected with the American Colonization Society and the settlement of formerly enslaved and free Black Americans in West Africa.

The similarity is therefore a direct visual reminder of Liberia's unusual founding history.

A short history of Liberia

Liberia's modern history began with the settlement of Black Americans on the West African coast in the nineteenth century. The settlement developed into the Republic of Liberia, which declared independence in 1847.

The country later experienced political tensions, military rule and two devastating civil wars before returning to democratic government.

History timeline

1816 — American Colonization Society

The American Colonization Society was formed to support the resettlement of free Black Americans in Africa.

1822 — Settlement at Cape Mesurado

Settlers established a permanent colony on the site of modern Monrovia.

1824 — Monrovia named

The settlement was named Monrovia in honour of US President James Monroe.

1847 — Independence

Liberia declared independence on 26 July 1847 and adopted its own constitution.

1848 — First president inaugurated

Joseph Jenkins Roberts became Liberia's first president.

Late 19th century — Expansion of state authority

The Liberian government expanded its control inland, although relations with indigenous communities were often unequal and contested.

20th century — International economic links

Rubber, shipping, mining and foreign investment became increasingly important to Liberia's economy.

1944 — William Tubman becomes president

Tubman began a long presidency marked by infrastructure development, foreign investment and the Open Door policy.

1971 — Tubman dies

William Tolbert succeeded him and introduced some political and economic reforms.

1980 — Military coup

Samuel Doe seized power, ending the Americo-Liberian political order.

1989 — First Liberian Civil War

Rebellion against Doe's government developed into a prolonged civil war.

1990 — Samuel Doe killed

Doe was captured and killed during the conflict.

1997 — Charles Taylor elected

Charles Taylor won presidential elections after the civil war period.

1999 — Second Liberian Civil War

A new rebellion began against Taylor's government.

2003 — Taylor leaves office

Taylor resigned and went into exile after international and regional pressure.

2005 — Ellen Johnson Sirleaf elected

Johnson Sirleaf became Africa's first elected female head of state.

2014–16 — Ebola epidemic

Liberia was one of the countries worst affected by the West African Ebola outbreak.

2018 — George Weah becomes president

Former footballer George Weah won the presidency.

2024 — Joseph Boakai becomes president

Joseph Boakai took office after defeating George Weah in the 2023 election.

The founding of Liberia

Liberia's founding was linked to the idea of resettling free Black Americans in Africa. The American Colonization Society helped establish settlements along the West African coast from 1822.

The settlers developed institutions based partly on American political traditions, eventually creating the independent Republic of Liberia.

Monrovia

Monrovia is Liberia's capital. It was named after US President James Monroe and developed around the original settlement at Cape Mesurado.

The city is Liberia's main political, commercial and cultural centre.

Americo-Liberian society

For much of Liberia's early history, political power was concentrated among the Americo-Liberian elite descended from settlers from the United States and the Caribbean.

This created major social and political divisions with Liberia's many indigenous communities.

Indigenous peoples of Liberia

Liberia is home to numerous indigenous ethnic groups, including the Kpelle, Bassa, Gio, Mano, Kru, Grebo and Loma, among others.

These communities have their own languages, traditions and histories and form the majority of Liberia's population.

William Tubman

William V. S. Tubman was Liberia's longest-serving president, governing from 1944 to 1971. His administration promoted foreign investment, infrastructure and a policy known as the Open Door.

He also introduced reforms intended to bring indigenous Liberians more fully into the political system, although inequalities remained.

The 1980 coup

In 1980, Master Sergeant Samuel Doe led a military coup that overthrew and killed President William Tolbert.

The coup ended the long period of Americo-Liberian political dominance and began a turbulent era of military rule and civil conflict.

The Liberian Civil Wars

Liberia suffered two major civil wars between 1989 and 2003. The conflicts caused enormous loss of life, displacement and destruction of infrastructure.

Regional organisations, neighbouring countries and international peacekeeping forces became heavily involved in efforts to end the fighting.

Ellen Johnson Sirleaf

In 2005, Ellen Johnson Sirleaf was elected president, becoming Africa's first elected female head of state.

Her presidency focused on rebuilding institutions, attracting investment, reducing corruption and recovering from the civil wars.

George Weah

George Weah, a former international football star, was elected president in 2017 and took office in 2018.

His election was notable because of his extraordinary transition from global sporting fame to national political leadership.

Liberia's geography

Liberia has a long Atlantic coastline with sandy beaches, lagoons and mangrove areas. Inland, the landscape rises into rolling hills and forested highlands.

Major rivers include the St Paul, St John, Cestos and Cavalla.

Liberia's tropical forests

Liberia contains some of the largest remaining areas of Upper Guinean rainforest in West Africa.

These forests support exceptional biodiversity and are important for climate regulation, water resources and local communities.

Mount Wuteve

Mount Wuteve in Lofa County is Liberia's highest mountain, rising to roughly 1,440 metres.

Although modest by global standards, Liberia's highlands form an important part of the country's inland landscape.

Liberia's climate

Liberia has a tropical climate with high temperatures and a pronounced wet season. Coastal areas receive especially heavy rainfall.

The long rainy season supports the country's forests and agriculture.

Liberia's wildlife

Liberia's forests support species including forest elephants, pygmy hippos, chimpanzees, leopards and many rare birds.

Habitat loss, hunting and development create ongoing conservation challenges.

Liberian culture

Liberian culture combines the traditions of many indigenous peoples with influences brought by settlers from the United States and Caribbean.

Music, dance, storytelling, textiles and food vary considerably between communities.

Liberian languages

English is Liberia's official language, while numerous indigenous languages are spoken across the country. Liberian English varieties are widely used in everyday communication.

The country's linguistic diversity reflects its many ethnic communities.

Liberian food

Liberian cuisine includes rice, cassava, plantains, palm oil, fish, chicken and spicy stews.

Rice is a particularly important staple, while cassava and plantain are common across the country.

Rubber in Liberia

Rubber became one of Liberia's most important export commodities during the twentieth century. The country developed major rubber plantations and attracted foreign investment.

Rubber remains an important agricultural product.

Iron ore and mining

Liberia has significant deposits of iron ore and other minerals. Mining has played an important role in the country's economy and export earnings.

The sector has also faced challenges related to infrastructure, employment, environmental protection and global commodity prices.

Liberia's economy

Liberia's economy depends on agriculture, mining, rubber, forestry, services and international assistance.

The country has made substantial progress since the civil wars but continues to face development challenges.

The Ebola epidemic

Liberia was one of the countries most severely affected by the 2014–16 West African Ebola epidemic. Thousands of people died and health services came under extreme pressure.

The outbreak also had major social and economic consequences.

Liberia today

Modern Liberia is a democratic republic with a young population and a strong connection to the United States through its unusual founding history.

Rebuilding infrastructure, expanding education and healthcare, developing natural resources sustainably and creating jobs remain important national priorities.

Why is Liberia's flag interesting?

Liberia's flag is one of the clearest examples of a national flag shaped by another country's history. Its 11 stripes and single white star echo the United States while representing Liberia's own independence and identity.

The flag therefore tells part of Liberia's founding story before you even know the country's history.
