About Armenia

Armenia is a small, landlocked country in the South Caucasus, between Europe and Asia. It is bordered by Turkey, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Iran and has a mountainous landscape with high plateaus, valleys and volcanic peaks.

Yerevan is the capital and largest city. Armenia has a long history stretching back thousands of years and has developed a distinctive cultural identity shaped by its language, Christian tradition, architecture, music and literature.

The flag of Armenia

Armenia's national flag is a horizontal tricolour of red, blue and orange. The three bands are equal in size and run across the entire flag.

A simple flag with a complicated history.
The modern tricolour is relatively simple visually, but its colours became important symbols of Armenian national identity during the modern independence movement.

Under Armenia's constitutional description, the red is associated with the Armenian Highland, the Armenian people's continued struggle for survival, the Christian faith and independence. Blue represents the will to live under peaceful skies, while orange represents the creative talent and hard-working nature of the Armenian people.

What do the colours on Armenia's flag mean?

The three colours have several layers of meaning. Red is linked to the Armenian Highland, survival, Christianity and the struggle for independence. Blue represents peaceful skies and the aspiration to live in peace. Orange represents the creativity and hard-working character of the Armenian people.

These meanings make the flag more than a simple national colour scheme: the tricolour connects the landscape, history and people of Armenia.

A short history of Armenia

Armenia has one of the oldest recorded histories in the South Caucasus. Ancient Armenian kingdoms developed in the region, including the Kingdom of Urartu and later the kingdoms associated with the Armenian highlands.

Armenia is especially notable for its Christian history. In the early fourth century, traditionally dated to 301 AD, the Kingdom of Armenia adopted Christianity as its state religion under King Tiridates III and Gregory the Illuminator.

Over the centuries Armenian territories came under the influence or control of larger empires, including the Byzantine, Persian, Arab, Ottoman and Russian empires. Despite these changes, Armenian language, religion and cultural traditions remained important elements of national identity.

Modern Armenia declared independence from the Soviet Union in 1991 following the collapse of the USSR.

Armenia's flag history

The red, blue and orange tricolour became an important modern symbol of Armenian national identity. A similar tricolour was used by the short-lived First Republic of Armenia after the First World War, before Armenia became part of the Soviet Union.

1918 — First Republic of Armenia

Armenia became an independent republic and adopted a red, blue and orange tricolour.

1920s–1991 — Soviet period

Armenia became part of the Soviet Union and used Soviet-era flags rather than the independent tricolour.

1990–1991 — independence restored

The Armenian tricolour was restored as the national flag during the process of independence from the Soviet Union.

Today

The red, blue and orange flag remains a central symbol of the Republic of Armenia and Armenian national identity.

Geography of Armenia

Armenia is a mountainous country with no coastline. Much of the land lies at high elevation, and the landscape includes volcanic mountains, deep valleys and high plateaus.

Armenia's neighbours

Armenia borders Georgia to the north, Azerbaijan to the east, Iran to the south and Turkey to the west. Its location has made it an important crossroads between the Caucasus, Anatolia and the Iranian plateau.

Mount Aragats is the highest mountain within Armenia's modern borders. Mount Ararat, however, remains an especially powerful symbol in Armenian culture despite lying in present-day Turkey.

People, language and culture

Armenian is the official language and has its own unique alphabet, traditionally created by Mesrop Mashtots in the early fifth century. The alphabet remains a major symbol of Armenian cultural identity.

Armenian culture includes a long tradition of religious architecture, stone carving, manuscript production, music and literature. The distinctive carved stone crosses known as khachkars are among the country's best-known cultural forms.

Armenia and Christianity

Christianity is deeply connected with Armenian history and national identity. Armenia is traditionally regarded as the first state to adopt Christianity as its official religion, and the Armenian Apostolic Church remains a major institution.

Medieval monasteries and churches can be found throughout the country, often in dramatic mountain settings. They provide a visible link between Armenia's modern identity and its ancient and medieval past.

Armenia's economy

Armenia has a mixed economy with important service, industry, agriculture and technology sectors. Mining has historically been significant, while information technology and other knowledge-based industries have become increasingly important.

Tourism also contributes to the economy, with visitors attracted by Yerevan, mountain landscapes, ancient monasteries, Lake Sevan and Armenia's archaeological and cultural heritage.

Lake Sevan

Lake Sevan is one of the largest high-altitude freshwater lakes in the world and is an important natural feature of Armenia. It sits at roughly 1,900 metres above sea level and is a major destination for recreation and tourism.

The lake is also economically and environmentally important, making water management and conservation significant issues for Armenia.

Armenia today

Modern Armenia is a parliamentary republic and an independent state in the South Caucasus. Yerevan combines modern urban life with a history stretching back thousands of years.

Armenia's location between major regional powers continues to influence its politics, economy and foreign relations, while its language, church, architecture and cultural traditions remain central to national identity.

Why is Armenia's flag important?

For Armenians, the tricolour represents more than the modern state. Its colours connect ideas of homeland, survival, peace, independence and the character of the Armenian people.

The flag also represents the restoration of Armenian independence after the Soviet period, linking the modern republic with the earlier independent Armenian republic of the early twentieth century.
