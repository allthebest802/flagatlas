About Sweden

Sweden is a Nordic country in northern Europe, occupying most of the eastern side of the Scandinavian Peninsula. It is known for forests, lakes, a long Baltic coastline, innovation and a distinctive Nordic cultural heritage.

The Swedish flag

The Swedish flag is a blue field with a yellow Nordic cross. The cross extends to the edges of the flag and reflects Sweden's long historical connection with the other Nordic countries.

Blue

The blue field is traditionally associated with the Swedish landscape and sky and forms the dominant background of the national flag.

Yellow

The yellow or gold cross is traditionally linked to the Swedish coat of arms and the country's royal heritage.

The Nordic cross

The Nordic cross is a shared design feature of the flags of Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Finland and Iceland, reflecting the historical and cultural connections between the Nordic countries.

Why the flag is distinctive

Sweden's flag is one of the world's best-known Nordic cross flags. Its bright yellow cross against a deep blue field gives it a simple but highly recognisable appearance.

A short history

Sweden developed from a collection of regional kingdoms into a unified medieval state. It became a major European power during the 17th century, later losing much of its empire. Sweden and Norway were united from 1814 to 1905, after which Sweden became a separate constitutional monarchy. Sweden has remained neutral in major European wars for much of the modern era and joined NATO in 2024.

History timeline

c. 800–1050 — Viking Age

Swedish Vikings travelled widely across the Baltic and along eastern European river routes.

12th–14th centuries — Medieval Sweden

Sweden developed into a more centralised kingdom and Christianity became firmly established.

1397–1523 — Kalmar Union

Sweden was part of the union linking Denmark, Norway and Sweden under a shared monarch.

1523 — Gustav Vasa

Gustav Vasa became king and Sweden left the Kalmar Union, establishing the foundations of the modern Swedish state.

17th century — Swedish Empire

Sweden became a major European military power and controlled territories around the Baltic Sea.

1700–1721 — Great Northern War

Sweden fought Russia and other European powers and eventually lost much of its position as a Baltic great power.

1814–1905 — Union with Norway

Sweden and Norway were joined in a personal union until Norway became independent in 1905.

1900s — Industrialisation

Sweden developed into a highly industrialised economy with major engineering, manufacturing and export industries.

1995 — EU membership

Sweden joined the European Union.

2022 — NATO application

Sweden applied to join NATO following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

2024 — NATO membership

Sweden became NATO's 32nd member on 7 March 2024.

Today

Sweden is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy and a highly developed Nordic economy.

Stockholm

Stockholm is Sweden's capital and largest city, built across numerous islands where Lake Mälaren meets the Baltic Sea. Its historic Gamla Stan is one of Europe's best-known old towns.

Gothenburg

Gothenburg is Sweden's second-largest city and a major port on the west coast. It has strong maritime, engineering and manufacturing traditions.

Kiruna

Kiruna lies far north within the Arctic Circle. It is famous for the nearby Icehotel, northern lights and the dramatic relocation of parts of the town because of mining.

Swedish Lapland

Swedish Lapland covers much of the far north and includes forests, mountains, lakes and Arctic landscapes. The region is home to the Sámi, the Indigenous people of northern Scandinavia.

Climate

Sweden has a varied climate. Southern areas are relatively mild, while northern Sweden has much colder winters and long summer daylight. Above the Arctic Circle, the midnight sun occurs in summer and polar night occurs in winter.

The northern lights

The aurora borealis can be seen in northern Sweden, particularly around Swedish Lapland during the darker months of the year.

Wildlife

Sweden has extensive forests and supports moose, brown bears, wolves, lynx, reindeer, beavers and many bird species. Its lakes and Baltic waters also support diverse aquatic wildlife.

Forests and lakes

Forests cover a large proportion of Sweden and the country contains tens of thousands of lakes. The landscape is particularly famous for its boreal forests and clear inland waters.

Economy

Sweden has a highly developed, export-oriented economy with strengths in engineering, manufacturing, technology, pharmaceuticals, telecommunications and services.

Innovation

Sweden has a strong reputation for innovation and technology. Companies and products associated with the country include IKEA, Volvo, Ericsson, Spotify and many other internationally recognised brands.

Engineering and industry

Engineering has played a central role in Sweden's economic development. Important industries include vehicles, machinery, telecommunications, mining, steel and advanced manufacturing.

Food

Swedish cuisine includes meatballs, gravlax, pickled herring, crispbread, potatoes and cinnamon buns. Traditional food is closely connected to the country's climate and seasonal produce.

Fika

Fika is a Swedish social tradition involving a break for coffee and something sweet or savoury. It is an important part of everyday Swedish culture.

Midsummer

Midsummer is one of Sweden's most important traditional celebrations. People gather around maypoles, eat seasonal food, sing, dance and celebrate the long summer days.

Sámi culture

The Sámi are the Indigenous people of northern Scandinavia. Traditional livelihoods include reindeer herding, and Sámi culture and languages remain an important part of northern Sweden.

Languages

Swedish is the main national language. Sweden also recognises several minority languages, including Sámi languages, Finnish, Meänkieli, Romani Chib and Yiddish.

Religion

Sweden has historically been Lutheran and the Church of Sweden remains the country's largest religious organisation, although modern Sweden is highly secular and religiously diverse.

Sport

Football, ice hockey, athletics and skiing are particularly popular. Sweden has also achieved international success in tennis, swimming and winter sports.

ABBA and music

Sweden has had an enormous influence on international popular music. ABBA became one of the world's most successful pop groups, while Swedish songwriters and producers have worked with major artists around the globe.

Swedish design

Swedish design is known for its emphasis on simplicity, functionality and clean forms. The influence can be seen in furniture, architecture, fashion and everyday products.

Why is Sweden's flag interesting?

The Swedish flag combines a traditional Nordic cross with the country's historic blue and yellow colours. Its simple design makes it instantly recognisable while linking Sweden to the wider Nordic family.
