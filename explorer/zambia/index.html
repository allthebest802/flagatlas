About Zambia

Zambia is a landlocked country in south-central Africa, bordered by the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Tanzania, Malawi, Mozambique, Zimbabwe, Botswana, Namibia and Angola.

The capital is Lusaka. Zambia is known for Victoria Falls, the Zambezi River, extensive wildlife areas and its important copper-mining industry.

The flag of Zambia

Zambia's national flag has a green field with three vertical stripes of red, black and orange near the lower-right corner. An orange eagle flies above the stripes.

About Zambia

Zambia is a landlocked country in south-central Africa, known for its spectacular waterfalls, wildlife, rivers and vast areas of wilderness. It takes its name from the Zambezi River, one of Africa's great rivers.

Lusaka is the capital and largest city. Zambia is famous for Victoria Falls, which it shares with Zimbabwe, as well as national parks such as South Luangwa, Kafue and Lower Zambezi.

Zambia at a glance

Capital: Lusaka   ·   Region: Southern Africa   ·   Currency: Zambian kwacha (ZMW)   ·   Official language: English   ·   Independence: 1964

The flag of Zambia

Zambia's national flag has a green field with three vertical stripes of red, black and orange near the lower-right corner. An orange eagle flies above the stripes.

The unusual position of the emblem makes the Zambian flag stand out from many other national flags. Green dominates the design, while the eagle and coloured stripes are grouped towards the fly end.

Flag symbolism

Green represents the country's rich natural resources and vegetation. Red represents the struggle for freedom, black represents the people of Zambia, and orange represents the country's mineral wealth.

The eagle represents the people's ability to rise above the country's difficulties and challenges.

The eagle and the colours

The eagle is placed above the three coloured stripes rather than in the centre of the flag. This gives Zambia one of Africa's most distinctive national flag designs. The combination of green, red, black and orange also reflects the country's natural wealth, people, independence struggle and mineral resources.

Flag history

1964 — IndependenceZambia became independent from British colonial rule on 24 October 1964 and adopted its distinctive green flag with an eagle.
1964 onward — National symbolsThe flag's colours and eagle became closely associated with the new independent Zambian state.
1997 — Minor design changesThe proportions and appearance of some flag elements were adjusted, while the basic design remained unchanged.
TodayThe green flag with the orange eagle and three stripes remains the national flag of Zambia.

A short history of Zambia

The region of modern Zambia has been inhabited for thousands of years by communities with diverse languages and cultures. During the nineteenth century, European explorers and missionaries increasingly travelled through the area, including David Livingstone.

The territory later became part of British colonial rule as Northern Rhodesia. Nationalist movements grew after the Second World War, leading to independence in 1964 under President Kenneth Kaunda.

Lusaka

Lusaka is Zambia's capital and largest city. It is the country's political, commercial and transport centre and has grown rapidly as Zambia's population and economy have expanded.

Victoria Falls

Victoria Falls, known locally as Mosi-oa-Tunya or 'The Smoke That Thunders', lies on the Zambezi River on the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe. It is one of the world's most spectacular waterfalls and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The Zambezi River

The Zambezi is one of Africa's great rivers and forms an important part of Zambia's geography. It flows through or along several countries before reaching the Indian Ocean in Mozambique.

Geography

Zambia is largely made up of elevated plateau country with broad river valleys and woodland. Major waterways include the Zambezi, Kafue and Luangwa rivers, while Lake Tanganyika lies along part of the country's northern border.

Climate

Zambia has a tropical seasonal climate with a wet season and a dry season. Temperatures vary with altitude, and the dry winter months are particularly important for wildlife viewing because animals often gather around water sources.

Wildlife

Zambia is one of Africa's outstanding wildlife destinations. Its protected areas support elephants, lions, leopards, hippos, crocodiles, buffalo, wild dogs, giraffes and numerous antelope species.

South Luangwa National Park

South Luangwa National Park is famous for its wildlife and walking safaris. The Luangwa River provides an important water source and supports large populations of hippos, elephants and other animals.

Kafue National Park

Kafue National Park is one of Africa's largest national parks. Its huge area includes woodland, floodplains and wetlands and supports an exceptionally wide range of wildlife.

Lower Zambezi

The Lower Zambezi region is known for its beautiful river scenery and wildlife. Canoeing and boating on the Zambezi offer visitors a different way to experience elephants, hippos and other animals.

People and language

Zambia is home to many ethnic groups and languages. English is the official language and is widely used in government, education and business, while Bemba, Nyanja, Tonga, Lozi and many other local languages are widely spoken.

Culture and traditions

Zambian culture is highly diverse, with traditional ceremonies playing an important role in many communities. The Kuomboka ceremony of the Lozi people is particularly famous and marks the seasonal movement of the Litunga from the Barotse floodplain to higher ground.

Food

A staple of Zambian cuisine is nshima, a thick maize meal commonly eaten with vegetables, beans, meat or fish. Maize is an important part of everyday food and agriculture across the country.

Economy

Zambia's economy has long depended heavily on copper mining. Agriculture, energy, construction, manufacturing, services and tourism are also important, with diversification remaining a major economic goal.

Zambia and copper

Zambia is one of Africa's major copper producers. The Copperbelt in the north has been central to the country's industrial development and remains one of the most important mining regions in Africa.

A landlocked country

Zambia has no coastline, but its rivers and lakes give it extensive freshwater resources. Its central location means it shares borders with eight countries: Tanzania, Malawi, Mozambique, Zimbabwe, Botswana, Namibia, Angola and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Why is Zambia's flag interesting?

Zambia's flag is unusual because almost all of its design is concentrated towards the fly end rather than the centre. The orange eagle appears above the three vertical stripes, while the large green field represents Zambia's natural environment.
