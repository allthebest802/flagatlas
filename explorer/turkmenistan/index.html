About Turkmenistan

Turkmenistan is a Central Asian country dominated by the vast Karakum Desert. It is famous for its distinctive national flag, ancient Silk Road cities, Akhal-Teke horses and huge natural gas reserves.

The Turkmenistan flag

Turkmenistan's flag is a green field with a vertical red carpet stripe near the hoist. The stripe contains five carpet guls, olive branches, and a crescent and five stars. It is one of the most detailed national flags in the world.

Flag colours and symbolism

Green and the crescent are associated with Islam and Turkmen identity. The red carpet stripe represents the country's traditional carpet-making heritage. The five carpet patterns represent the country's five major tribal groups, while the olive branches symbolise neutrality and peace. The five stars represent the country's five provinces.

Flag history

The current national flag was adopted after independence in 1991, following the dissolution of the Soviet Union. It has been modified over time, including changes to the arrangement and symbolism of the stars and carpet motifs, but the green, red carpet stripe and detailed national symbolism have remained central.

A short history

The territory of modern Turkmenistan has been shaped by ancient Iranian and Central Asian civilisations, the Silk Road, Turkic migrations and Islamic empires. It became part of the Russian Empire in the 19th century and later the Soviet Union before declaring independence in 1991.

Ashgabat

Ashgabat is Turkmenistan's capital and largest city. It is famous for monumental white-marble buildings, broad avenues and grand public spaces, creating one of the world's most unusual modern capital-city landscapes.

Karakum Desert

The Karakum Desert covers most of Turkmenistan and is one of the largest sandy deserts in the world. Its name means 'Black Sand' in Turkic languages.

Darvaza gas crater

The Darvaza gas crater, often nicknamed the 'Door to Hell', is a large burning natural-gas crater in the Karakum Desert. It became famous internationally because of its long-running flames and remote desert setting.

Merv

Ancient Merv was one of the great cities of the medieval Islamic world. Its ruins reflect thousands of years of settlement and its importance on the Silk Road.

Köneürgenç

Köneürgenç was an important medieval centre of Khwarazm. Its surviving monuments include mausoleums, minarets and other Islamic architectural remains.

Caspian Sea

Turkmenistan has a coastline on the Caspian Sea. The port of Türkmenbaşy provides an important link between the country and maritime trade routes across the Caspian.

Geography

Turkmenistan borders Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Afghanistan and Iran, with the Caspian Sea to the west. Most of the country is desert, while the Kopet Dag mountains rise along the southern border.

Climate

Turkmenistan has a strongly continental desert climate. Summers can be extremely hot and dry, while winters can be cold, particularly away from the southern coast.

Wildlife

Desert and mountain habitats support goitered gazelles, saiga, wild sheep, wolves, foxes and many desert birds. Conservation is particularly important for species adapted to the country's arid landscapes.

Akhal-Teke horses

The Akhal-Teke is a famous Turkmen horse breed prized for its endurance, speed and distinctive metallic-looking coat. It is an important national symbol and part of Turkmen cultural heritage.

Turkmen carpets

Traditional Turkmen carpets are among the country's best-known cultural products. Their geometric patterns and tribal guls are so important that carpet motifs appear directly on the national flag.

Turkmen language

Turkmen is a Turkic language closely related to Turkish and Azerbaijani. The language was written using several scripts during the 20th century and now uses a Latin-based alphabet.

Religion

The majority of Turkmenistan's population is Muslim, predominantly Sunni. Islam is an important part of cultural identity, although the state maintains a strongly secular political system.

Food

Turkmen cuisine reflects Central Asian traditions and commonly includes plov, manty, kebab, flatbreads, soups and dishes based on lamb and vegetables. Tea is an important everyday drink.

Culture

Turkmen cultural traditions include horse breeding, carpet weaving, music, oral storytelling and distinctive national dress. Hospitality and respect for elders are important social values.

Economy

Turkmenistan's economy is heavily dependent on natural gas, with significant petroleum, chemical and agricultural sectors as well. The country has some of the world's largest natural-gas reserves.

Natural gas

Large gas fields, particularly in the east of the country, have made Turkmenistan a major energy producer. Gas exports are an important source of government revenue and foreign currency.

Agriculture

Despite its desert environment, irrigation allows agriculture to flourish in selected areas. Important products include cotton, wheat, melons and livestock. The Karakum Canal is a major irrigation project.

Permanent neutrality

Turkmenistan is unusual because it has a formal policy of permanent neutrality. The United Nations recognised this status in 1995, and neutrality remains a central feature of the country's foreign policy.

Why is the flag interesting?

Turkmenistan has one of the world's most detailed national flags. The vertical carpet stripe incorporates five traditional carpet guls, olive branches, a crescent and five stars, combining national, cultural and political symbolism in a single design.
