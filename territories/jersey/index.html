About Jersey

Jersey is the largest of the Channel Islands, lying in the English Channel in the Bay of Saint-Malo, just off the coast of Normandy in France. It is a British Crown Dependency — self-governing, and not part of the United Kingdom or the European Union — with its own parliament, laws and taxes.

Saint Helier is the capital. The reigning British monarch is head of state, and the United Kingdom is responsible for the island's defence and foreign affairs. Jersey issues its own currency, the Jersey pound, which circulates at par with pound sterling.

The flag of Jersey

Jersey's flag is a white field with a diagonal red saltire (a diagonal cross reaching corner to corner). In the upper part sits the badge of Jersey: a red shield with three golden lions, one above another, ensigned with a yellow crown.

The badge is a modern addition.
For a long time Jersey's flag was simply the plain red saltire on white. The shield of three lions and the crown were added by Royal Warrant in 1981 to distinguish it clearly as the flag of Jersey.

The three lions echo the traditional arms of Jersey, which are closely related to the historic royal arms of England and Normandy.

The red saltire and the three lions

The origin of Jersey's red saltire is not certain. One long-standing explanation is that it grew out of confusion with the cross of Saint Patrick used in the Union Flag; whatever its beginnings, the plain red-on-white saltire had become established as Jersey's flag by the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

The three golden lions are the same charge found on the arms of Jersey and are shared with the historic arms of the English crown and the Dukes of Normandy — a reminder of the island's deep links to both. Adding the crowned shield in 1981 gave Jersey a flag that could not be mistaken for any other.

A short history of Jersey

Jersey was part of the Duchy of Normandy, and when Duke William conquered England in 1066 the island came under the same ruler as England. In 1204, when King John lost mainland Normandy to the King of France, the Channel Islands chose to remain loyal to the English crown — and they have been attached to it ever since as the last remnant of the medieval duchy.

Over the centuries Jersey developed its own institutions and a large degree of self-government. During the Second World War the Channel Islands were occupied by German forces from 1940 to 1945 — the only part of the British Isles to be occupied.

Today Jersey is a prosperous, self-governing Crown Dependency, best known internationally as a financial centre.

The Jersey flag's history

Jersey's flag developed from a plain saltire into the crowned-badge design used today.

18th–19th century

The plain red saltire on a white field becomes established as Jersey's flag.

1979–1981

A distinctive design is agreed, adding the island's badge to the saltire.

1981 — official flag

The red saltire with the crowned shield of three lions is confirmed by Royal Warrant.

Today

The crowned-saltire flag is flown across the island.

Geography of Jersey

Jersey is a small island of about 118 square kilometres, tilted to catch the sun, with a plateau that slopes from higher ground in the north down to sandy bays in the south. Its large tidal range is among the greatest in the world, uncovering vast expanses of beach and rock at low tide.

Jersey's neighbours

The coast of Normandy, France, lies only about 22 km to the east, while Great Britain is roughly 160 km to the north. The other Channel Islands — including Guernsey, Alderney and Sark — lie to the north-west.

People, language and culture

English is the main language today, but Jersey's historic language is Jèrriais, a form of Norman French, and French long held an official role in the island's law and government. Place-names and family names across the island reflect this Norman heritage.

Jersey is known for its agricultural exports — the early "Jersey Royal" potato and the fawn-coloured Jersey breed of dairy cattle — as well as its role as an international finance centre.

Jersey today

Jersey governs itself through the States of Jersey, its parliament, and manages its own domestic affairs. Its economy is dominated by financial services, alongside tourism, agriculture and digital industries.

As a Crown Dependency, Jersey is neither part of the United Kingdom nor a member of the European Union, but maintains a close relationship with both.
