Flags beyond the 195 countries

As well as the flags of the world's sovereign nations, many territories, regions and constituent countries have flags of their own — from England, Scotland and Wales in the United Kingdom, to the Faroe Islands and Greenland (Denmark), Martinique and Réunion (France), and Hong Kong and Macau (China), to self-governing Taiwan. This section collects them together, grouped by the country each one belongs to.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a country and a territory?

A country is a fully sovereign, independent state — usually a member of the United Nations. A territory or region is governed by, or forms part of, a sovereign country, but often has its own distinct flag, culture and identity. England, Martinique and the Faroe Islands are examples.

Why isn't England one of the 195 countries?

England is a constituent country of the United Kingdom, which is the sovereign state. The UK — made up of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland — is the country that sits among the world's 195 nations, but each of its nations has its own flag.

Are these flags free to use?

The flag images on FlagAtlas are free to view and download, and many have a matching free printable to colour.
