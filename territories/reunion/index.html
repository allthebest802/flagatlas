Flag of Réunion

The Radiant Volcano — a red volcano and golden sun rays on a blue field.

The flag shown here is the Radiant Volcano, commonly known as Lo Mavéli or Lo Mahavéli. It features a red triangle representing Réunion's volcanic landscape, five golden rays spreading from its summit and a blue background. The design is widely used as a local symbol of the island.

The Radiant Volcano

The design was created by Guy Pignolet in 1975 and was selected in a 2003 competition organised by the Association Réunionnaise de Vexillologie. Several municipalities have subsequently displayed it, and it is widely used to represent Réunion.

Frequently asked questions

What does the flag of Réunion look like?

The Radiant Volcano design has a blue field, a red volcanic triangle and five yellow rays spreading from the volcano's summit.

What do the colours mean?

Red represents the volcano and strength, blue represents the sky and gentleness, and yellow represents the sun and clarity. The rays have also been described as symbolising the people who have come to live on the island from different parts of the world.

Is the Réunion flag official?

Réunion does not have a separate official flag recognised by the French state. The French tricolour is the official national flag. The Radiant Volcano is a widely used local flag and has been adopted for display by several municipalities.

Where is Réunion?

Réunion is a French overseas department and region in the Indian Ocean, east of Madagascar. The island is strongly associated with its volcanic landscape, including Piton de la Fournaise.
