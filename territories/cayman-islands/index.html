About the Cayman Islands

The Cayman Islands are a British Overseas Territory in the western Caribbean Sea, lying south of Cuba and north-west of Jamaica. The territory is made up of three islands: Grand Cayman, much the largest and home to the capital, together with the smaller Cayman Brac and Little Cayman to the north-east.

The Cayman Islands are self-governing in their internal affairs, with the reigning British monarch as head of state represented by a Governor, while the United Kingdom is responsible for defence and foreign affairs. George Town, on Grand Cayman, is the capital, and the local currency is the Cayman Islands dollar.

The flag of the Cayman Islands

The flag is a British Blue Ensign — a dark blue field with the Union Jack in the canton — defaced in the fly with the territory's coat of arms.

What the arms show.
The shield carries three green stars, one for each island, floating above wavy blue and white lines that represent the sea, with a gold lion on a red band at the top for the link with Britain. Above the shield sit a green turtle and a pineapple, and below runs the motto He hath founded it upon the seas.

The motto is taken from Psalm 24 and reflects the islands' seafaring history and their setting in the Caribbean.

The turtle, the stars and the motto

Almost every part of the arms points to the islands and the sea. The three stars are the three islands; the wavy blue and white is the Caribbean; the turtle recalls both the islands' old name, Las Tortugas, and their long history of turtling; and the pineapple reflects the historic link with Jamaica, with which the islands were once governed.

The gold lion at the top of the shield stands for the United Kingdom, while the motto He hath founded it upon the seas ties the whole design to the ocean that surrounds and sustains the islands.

A short history of the Cayman Islands

Christopher Columbus sighted the islands in 1503 and named them Las Tortugas after the many sea turtles in the surrounding waters. The name "Cayman" came later, from a word for the caiman or crocodile. The islands were uninhabited when Europeans arrived and were gradually settled, largely by people from Jamaica.

For much of their history the Cayman Islands were administered together with Jamaica. When Jamaica became independent in 1962, the Cayman Islands chose to remain under the British Crown and became a separate territory.

In the later twentieth century the islands developed into one of the world's leading offshore financial centres, and, together with tourism, this transformed them into one of the wealthier territories in the Caribbean.

Geography of the Cayman Islands

The three Cayman Islands are low-lying and flat, formed from coral and limestone rather than volcanic rock. Grand Cayman is famous for Seven Mile Beach and for the shallow, clear waters that have made the territory one of the world's premier scuba-diving destinations.

The islands' neighbours

Cuba lies to the north and Jamaica to the south-east, with the deep waters of the Cayman Trench — the deepest point in the Caribbean Sea — running just to the south of the islands.

People, language and culture

English is the official language, and the population is unusually international, with a large share of residents drawn from around the world by the islands' finance and tourism industries. Traditional Caymanian culture is rooted in the sea — in fishing, turtling and boatbuilding.

The green sea turtle remains a national symbol, celebrated on the flag and cared for at the islands' turtle centre, and the surrounding reefs and wall dives draw visitors from across the globe.

The Cayman Islands today

The Cayman Islands govern their own domestic affairs through an elected parliament and government, with a Governor representing the Crown. The territory is one of the largest offshore financial centres in the world, home to many banks and investment funds, and this — with high-end tourism — underpins a high standard of living.

As a British Overseas Territory, the Cayman Islands remain under UK sovereignty for defence and foreign affairs while managing their internal affairs themselves.
