About Bermuda

Bermuda is a British Overseas Territory in the North Atlantic Ocean, lying around 1,000 kilometres east of the coast of the United States. Despite its subtropical climate and beaches, it is not in the Caribbean. It is made up of a chain of around 180 small islands, the largest of which are joined by bridges and causeways.

Hamilton is the capital. Bermuda is self-governing in its internal affairs, with the reigning British monarch as head of state represented by a Governor, while the United Kingdom is responsible for defence and foreign affairs. Its currency, the Bermudian dollar, is pegged one-to-one to the US dollar.

The flag of Bermuda

Bermuda's flag is a British Red Ensign — a red field with the Union Jack in the canton — defaced in the fly with the territory's coat of arms. Most British territories use a blue ensign, which makes Bermuda's red one unusual.

What the arms show.
A red lion, standing upright, holds a shield depicting the Sea Venture foundering on rocks. The scene records the 1609 shipwreck that led directly to the settlement of the islands.

The Red Ensign with the Bermuda arms was adopted in 1910 and has represented the territory ever since.

The wreck of the Sea Venture

In 1609 the Sea Venture, the flagship of a fleet carrying settlers to the English colony at Jamestown in Virginia, was caught in a storm and deliberately run onto Bermuda's reefs to save those on board. The survivors spent months on the uninhabited islands before building new vessels and continuing to Virginia.

Reports of the wreck and the "still-vexed Bermoothes" are widely believed to have inspired William Shakespeare's play The Tempest. The shipwreck also opened the way to permanent English settlement, and the ship on Bermuda's coat of arms commemorates it.

A short history of Bermuda

Bermuda had no native population. The islands were sighted by the Spanish navigator Juan de Bermúdez in the early sixteenth century, and take their name from him, but were not settled at the time.

The wreck of the Sea Venture in 1609 led to permanent English settlement from 1612, making Bermuda one of the oldest English — later British — overseas possessions. Its parliament, established in 1620, is among the oldest legislatures in the world.

Over the following centuries Bermuda developed as a maritime and, later, tourism and financial centre, and today it is a prosperous, self-governing British Overseas Territory.

The Bermuda flag's history

Bermuda's flag follows the British ensign tradition, distinguished by its red field and the shipwreck arms.

British colonial era

Bermuda used British ensigns as part of the wider colonial flag tradition.

1910 — the red ensign

The Red Ensign defaced with Bermuda's coat of arms was adopted as the territory's flag.

Today

The red ensign with the shipwreck arms remains the flag of Bermuda.

Geography of Bermuda

Bermuda is one of the most isolated inhabited places on Earth, with the nearest land — Cape Hatteras in North Carolina — about 1,000 kilometres away. The islands are the tops of an ancient underwater volcano, capped with limestone, and are ringed by coral reefs that are among the northernmost in the world.

Bermuda's setting

The territory is famous for its pink-tinted sand beaches and turquoise water, and it lends its name to the so-called "Bermuda Triangle" of Atlantic folklore. The warm Gulf Stream keeps the climate mild for its latitude.

People, language and culture

English is the official language, and there is a long-established Portuguese-speaking community descended from Azorean immigrants. Bermudian culture blends British, African and Portuguese influences, seen in its music, food and the colourful Gombey dancers.

Bermuda has given the world the "Bermuda shorts," worn with jackets and knee socks as smart business dress on the islands — a distinctive local custom.

Bermuda today

Bermuda governs its own domestic affairs through its parliament, with a Governor representing the Crown. It is one of the world's leading centres for international insurance and reinsurance, and this — together with tourism — underpins one of the highest income levels of any territory.

As a British Overseas Territory, Bermuda remains under UK sovereignty for defence and foreign affairs while running its internal affairs itself.
