Flag of Niue

A yellow field with the Union Jack and five stars.

The flag of Niue has a bright yellow field with the Union Jack in the canton, to which five yellow stars have been added — a large one on a blue disc in the centre, and four smaller ones on the arms of the cross. Niue is a self-governing island nation in the South Pacific, in free association with New Zealand.

About the Niue flag

The flag was adopted in 1975, when Niue became self-governing. Unusually, its Union Jack is modified: the bright yellow field is said to represent the warm feelings of the Niuean people towards New Zealand and the island's sunshine. The large gold star on a blue disc in the centre stands for Niue and its self-government, while the four smaller stars represent the Southern Cross, echoing the link with New Zealand.

Frequently asked questions

What does the flag look like?

A yellow field with the Union Jack in the top-left, with five yellow stars added — a big one on a blue disc in the centre and four smaller ones on the cross.

What do the stars mean?

The central star = Niue and its self-government; the four smaller ones = the Southern Cross, the link to New Zealand.

Is Niue part of New Zealand?

It's a self-governing nation in free association with New Zealand; Niueans are NZ citizens. Not a separate UN country, so it sits here under Territories & Regions.

When was the flag adopted?

In 1975, when Niue became self-governing in free association with New Zealand.
