Oldest National Flags

Explore the world's oldest national flag traditions, including Denmark and other long-established designs. The definition of 'oldest' matters.

Denmark and the Dannebrog

Denmark's Dannebrog is widely recognised as the world's oldest continuously used national flag tradition. Guinness World Records identifies it as the oldest national flag still in use. The familiar red field and white Scandinavian cross have become one of the defining designs of northern Europe. citeturn0search2

The 1219 legend

A famous Danish tradition says the flag fell from the sky during the Battle of Valdemar in 1219. The story is part of Danish national tradition, but it should be distinguished from documentary evidence for the flag's later historical use.

Why 'oldest' needs a definition

There is a big difference between the earliest surviving evidence of a symbol, the date a current design was formally adopted, and continuous use as a national flag. A careful FlagAtlas comparison therefore avoids pretending that one date answers every question.

Other ancient flag traditions

Austria, Latvia, Sweden, Scotland and several other countries have flag traditions reaching back many centuries. In many cases their designs evolved from medieval dynastic, military, regional or religious symbols.

The Nordic cross connection

Denmark's white cross on red became a powerful model for later Nordic flags. Sweden, Norway, Finland and Iceland all use the offset Scandinavian cross, while the Faroe Islands use a related design.

A useful way to compare old flags

When researching an old flag, ask: when is the earliest evidence, when was the present design standardised, and has the design remained in continuous national use? These questions give a much clearer picture than a simple 'oldest' ranking.
